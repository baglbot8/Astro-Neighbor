# Astro Neighbor — Architecture Contract

Godot **4.7.1**, Forward+ renderer, GDScript only, **no external assets** (every mesh, texture, sound is generated by code in this repo — Python scripts under `tools/gen/` may pre-bake WAV/PNG files into `assets/`).
Target: 1280×720, 60 fps on Apple Silicon.

Read this whole file before writing code. If you must change a contract, update this file AND every caller.

## 1. The game in one paragraph
You are a small astronaut in a big bubble helmet, living on a planet you can walk all the way round (spherical gravity) — home is 12 m in radius, 75 m round, 18 s at walking pace — so the horizon is a visible arc in every shot and every stroll is a lap of a world. Each neighbour lives on a world of their own: **Zorp** (a lavender alien in a violet hollow with glowing blue rivers), **Bolt** (a robot in a chrome yard under a ring), **Fen** (a slate-blue alien on a terracotta flat where the sun never climbs past 11 degrees), **Grig** (on a dry staircase of chalk terraces) and **Vela** (on a still frost world). A rocket carries you between them in one continuous flight — your planet shrinks below, the next one grows ahead (STYLE_GUIDE R2.5). The air is too thin for a blue sky, so the sky is always space: stars at noon, a dusty glow hugging the limb, and your neighbours' worlds hanging overhead as both scenery and map (R2.1). The cast is aliens, robots and one fellow astronaut — never animals. The big **Starport Plaza** hub planet (21 m) carries the shared buildings: the Town Hall, the decoration store (**Cosmo Depot**), the clothes store (**Suit-Up**), and an open event space. Built today on top of that: collect stardust, decorate your planet with alien/robot/space-themed furniture, do favors for neighbours to earn random decorations, and spend at the hub. Several of those systems mirror another game closely and are under review — §1.1 says which, and what is actually decided. It plays on desktop and in a phone browser in landscape (R2.10).

**The quality bar is our own, and most of it is measured.** *Polish*: nothing floats, clips, pops or z-fights; under 14 ms average frame time at 1280x720; zero console errors or warnings (QUALITY_BAR "Automatic FAIL triggers"). *Warmth*: a warm sun on matte pastel ground, idle life on every character, and sound + motion + feedback on every interaction. *Readability*: every silhouette reads from the gameplay camera, on a phone as well as on a monitor. *Cute*: chibi proportions and simple faces, to the measured numbers in STYLE_GUIDE "Character rules". *Palette*: every daytime planet frame passes the gates, including: whole frame saturation p90 <= 0.68, saturation mean 0.36-0.48, no dominant swatch above S 0.60; ground crop value mean 0.66-0.76, luma p05 0.30-0.42, luma range >= 0.50, blown < 5%; and luma p95 <= 0.93 (STYLE_GUIDE R2.6; QUALITY_BAR "REVISION 2 gates" and "measure the GROUND"). "As good as some other game" is not a gate. Those numbers are.

### 1.1 Identity: what Astro Neighbor is not
Added 2026-09-10. The project started out Animal Crossing-inspired, and until this date these docs named *Animal Crossing: New Horizons* as the quality target — so every new feature drifted back toward it. The user has asked to differentiate:

> *"I still think components of the game are still a bit too animal crossing like including the voices and jingle at the start of the game. I want to make this game feel more unique so people dont call it an animal crossing rip off."*
> *"This is AC inspired originally but we want to differentiate."*

The Animal Crossing screenshots in `reference/` (`AC Characters.png` and its `.avif` copy, `AC Reference 2-6`) are retired along with that target: they are not a target and not a ruler. A few numbers in STYLE_GUIDE were first measured off them (the tonal range, the saturation tail, eye spacing, mouth width). Those numbers are recorded there, they are the rule, and nothing needs re-measuring against the screenshots.

Below are the tells: shipped systems that mirror that game closely enough to read as borrowed. Each has a status. **Only the two sound items are decided.** Everything else is OPEN (being designed, options going to the user) or UNDER REVIEW (the user is reconsidering it; no replacement has been chosen). This list is not a decision to cut anything, and it is not a freeze. What it means for a builder:
* **Never add a DECIDED item back**: no per-letter voice sound (a sound tied to individual typed characters, or a pitched, speech-like syllable babble — "animalese"), no bouncy mallet / whistle start jingle. A critic fails a change that adds one (QUALITY_BAR step 4). Zorp's comms voice, the shared "doot" for everyone else, and the original title track below are what now ship; a change is only failed for adding a new per-letter/animalese voice event or a new bouncy mallet/whistle jingle, not for these already-shipped replacements.
* **The OPEN and UNDER REVIEW systems keep shipping and keep working.** If your change extends one of them, say so in your report, so the orchestrator can weigh it against the pending decision. That is a flag, not a block.

**What IS Astro Neighbor's own — lean into these.** When a feature needs a hook, reach here first.
* **Walking all the way round a tiny planet.** Home is 18 s round at walking pace, and the horizon must read as a clean arc (STYLE_GUIDE "Shape language corrections"). The idea to lean into is that on a world this small, day and night can be a PLACE you walk to, not only a time you wait for. **Honest status: that is not what ships.** `environment.gd` orbits the sun and moons in the player's local frame ("noon is always overhead and dusk always sits on the horizon wherever you stand on the sphere"), so walking round the planet does not change the light. The nearest built thing is Fen's Long Dusk, whose `sun_peak_elev_deg` of 11.0 keeps the sun on the horizon all day. Changing the sun model is not decided.
* **Rocket travel.** One continuous journey, never a cut: your planet shrinks below, the destination grows ahead (R2.5).
* **Neighbours who live on their own worlds.** Visiting someone is a trip, not a walk across town, and each world is theirs: its own biome, music, sun height and terrain grammar (NEXT_WORLDS.md).
* **Aliens and robots, not animals.** The cast differs in shape — eye stalks, screens, plates, a lander-pad foot — not in species (CAST_VARIETY.md).
* **The astronaut in the helmet.** A big bubble helmet on a neck ring, an opaque navy visor with no face behind it, personality carried by body language, and a jetpack (R2.2, R2.7, R2.8).
* **The space sky.** Stars in daylight, a thin dusty limb, and the neighbouring worlds always overhead, correctly lit, as scenery and as navigation (R2.1).

Sound rules for all new audio work live in STYLE_GUIDE "Sound identity".

## 3. Scene assembly (`src/world/world.gd`)
`world.tscn` builds the planet named by `GameState.current_planet_id`. Fixed node paths (Director & systems rely on them):
```
/root/World/Planet         Planet
/root/World/Environment    from src/world/environment.tscn
/root/World/Player         Player (extends PlanetBody)
/root/World/CameraRig      from src/player/camera_rig.tscn (contains the active Camera3D)
```

Any node that needs the planet: `get_tree().get_first_node_in_group("planet")` or `get_node("/root/World/Planet")`. Planet adds itself to group `"planet"` (planet builder: do this in `_ready`). Player adds itself to group `"player"`.

## 4. Spherical world rules
* Planet center = `Planet.global_position` (always at origin in world.tscn; planet never rotates/scales).
* `dir` = unit vector from center. **All persistent positions are stored as local positions relative to the planet** (so they survive nothing moving anyway, but keep it consistent).
* `Planet.height_at(dir)` MUST equal the actual mesh/collision height along `dir`. Props, decorations, NPC feet all use it.
* `PlanetBody` (src/core/planet_body.gd) handles gravity & orientation. Player and NPCs extend it. Read it.
* Model convention: characters/props face **-Z**, up is **+Y**, origin at the **ground contact point**.
* Camera-relative movement: `forward = camera.forward projected on tangent plane`, `right = forward.cross(up)`.
* Scale: player is ~1.4 m tall (helmet top). Home radius **12 m**, neighbor planets **10.5 m**, hub **21 m** (R2.11 - smaller starting worlds so more of the player's own decorating is on screen at once).
* **Home's radius is not a constant.** It resolves from `GameState.home_planet_size` (an integer level, default 0) through `PlanetData.HOME_RADII` at load, so the future "expand your planet" upgrade is a data change. Only the shared `home.tres` resolves this way - a duplicated resource keeps its authored value, which is what lets the title screen keep its own preview globe.
* **Size fields in a `.tres` are authored at a 16 m reference** (`PlanetData.REFERENCE_RADIUS`) and rescaled at load: vertical amplitudes and depths x r/16, horizontal radii x r/16, scatter counts x (r/16)^2 so props-per-square-metre is radius-independent. Noise frequency is already `hill_frequency / radius`, so scaling only the horizontal would turn a crater into a canyon on a small world. Walk speed 4.2 m/s, run 7 m/s.

## 5. Autoload APIs (already implemented — read the files)
* `EventBus` — all signals. `EventBus.is_modal_open()` gates gameplay input.
* `GameState` — persistent data (dictionaries only). `add_stardust`, `add_item`, `remove_item`, `add_placed_decoration`, `npc_data`, `favors`, `player_style`, `flags`.
* `Catalog` — item definitions. Decoration builder provides `res://src/decorations/decoration_catalog.gd` with `get_items() -> Array[Dictionary]`; hub builder provides `res://src/hub/clothing_catalog.gd` likewise. `Catalog.get_item(id)`, `random_reward_decoration()`, `store_items(kind)`.
* `SaveManager.save_game() / load_game() / has_save()`.
* `AudioManager.play_sfx(name)`, `play_sfx_at(name, pos)`, `play_music(track)`. Dialogue voice (§1.1, STYLE_GUIDE "Sound identity") is ONE entry-point pair shared by both speakers, dispatched internally on who is talking: `comms_open_line(voice, line, closes_turn)`, `comms_reveal(visible_chars)` (called every frame with how much of the line is typed), `comms_close_line(cut)` and `comms_hold(paused)` (pause/resume without ending the line). `comms_voice_for(profile, speaker, line_index)` resolves the caller's `voice` string first, unchanged from before — it still returns a neighbour's own id (e.g. `"bolt"`) or a legacy profile. Inside the four `comms_*` functions, `voice == "zorp"` runs the original comms scheduler (key-up, 1-2 voiced gestures per phrase, key-down squelch, "over"); any other non-empty `voice` runs the doot path instead (`DOOT_FILES`, `DOOT_LETTERS_PER_TICK = 2`, `DOOT_DB`): no key-up/key-down framing (`DOOT_FRAMING_ENABLED` — one const, default off), just one shared, neutral tone chosen pseudo-randomly from 4 pitch variants as letters are revealed, counting only alnum characters so spaces/punctuation never trigger one; `voice == ""` types in silence either way. `play_voice_blip(profile)` still exists as a compatibility wrapper — Zorp's gesture, or a doot for anyone else; its one remaining caller is `showcase/audio_board.gd:88`.
* `SceneRouter.go_to_planet(id)`, `go_to_space(from_id)`, `go_to_title()`, `start_game()`.
* `Director` — automated input for tests (see file header).

## 6. Cross-domain contracts (APIs other builders will call)
### Player (`src/player/player.gd`, class_name Player, extends PlanetBody)
```
var input_enabled: bool            # false while modal UI open (listen to EventBus.ui_modal_opened/closed)
func face_toward(world_pos: Vector3)   # snap-turn toward an NPC when talking
func play_emote(name: String)          # "wave", "happy", "think", "dance", "surprised"
func set_carry_item(item_id: String)   # show a held item over the head (favor deliveries) ; "" clears
signal interact_target_changed(target: Interactable)  # HUD shows prompt
```
Player finds the nearest `Interactable` (group "interactables") within its `reach`, calls `interactable.interact(self)` on `interact` action.
Jump exists (space). Player must never fall through terrain or float; landing squash/stretch, dust puff, shadow blob (or real shadow).

### Camera (`src/player/camera_rig.gd`)
Third-person follow camera aligned to the planet up vector, high and close, looking down on the player with the planet's horizon arc in frame: elevated 28°, distance **7.4 m** (zoom 4.4-11.4) on desktop and **8.6 m at 34°** (zoom 5.0-13.0) on mobile, selected through `dist_default()`/`pitch_default_deg()` and re-applied on `Platform.mode_changed`. Full zoom-out is additionally capped at 0.75x the planet's radius, so it stays sane on a 10.5 m world and on a home planet that can grow to 18 m. Touch callers use `add_look_px()` / `add_zoom()` and must NOT pre-apply sensitivity or invert - the rig does that itself, yaw with `camera_left/right`, smooth lag, never clips into terrain (spring arm). Exposes `func get_camera() -> Camera3D` and `func focus_on(pos: Vector3, duration: float)` for dialogue framing; `func release_focus()`.

### Dialogue UI (`src/ui/dialogue/dialogue_box.gd`, autoload-free; instanced by HUD as `/root/World/HUD/DialogueBox`) — UI BUILDER provides:
```
func show_lines(speaker: String, lines: Array, voice_profile: String = "alien", accent: Color = Color("#5b7cff")) -> void   # await dialogue_box.finished
func show_choice(prompt: String, options: Array) -> int          # await; returns index, -1 on cancel
func hide_box()
signal finished
signal choice_made(index: int)
```
Typewriter text in a rounded Moonstone panel with a name tag (the box's *form* is OPEN — see §1.1), voice via the same comms entry points (`AudioManager.comms_open_line` / `comms_reveal` / `comms_close_line` — §5): dialogue_box.gd itself is unchanged by the 2026-09-10 voice revision — it calls the same four functions for every speaker, and `AudioManager` decides internally whether that means Zorp's framed radio transmission (key-up, one or two voiced gestures per phrase, never per letter, measured ~2 gestures/s, a key-down squelch, a two-beep "over" on the last line of a turn) or, for everyone else, an unframed shared doot roughly every 2 revealed letters — STYLE_GUIDE "Sound identity". Advance with `interact`/`ui_accept`, skip-to-end on second press (which also cuts whichever voice path is open). Emits `EventBus.ui_modal_opened("dialogue")` / closed.
Character builder's `DialogueRunner` (src/dialogue/) wraps this: `Dialogue.say(npc, lines)` etc.

### Favors (`src/favors/favor_system.gd`, class_name FavorSystem, Node added by world or NPC)
Generates favors from templates, stores in `GameState.favors`, listens to inventory/collectibles, emits `EventBus.favor_*`. Reward = `Catalog.random_reward_decoration()` + stardust.
Types: `fetch` (collect N of a collectible on that NPC's planet), `deliver` (carry an item to another NPC — spans planets), `bring` (bring N of a material from anywhere).
Status: **UNDER REVIEW** (§1.1). This contract is what ships and it keeps working; if your change extends it, flag that in your report (§1.1).

### Decorations
`decoration_catalog.gd` → `get_items()` (25+ items, see Catalog format). Each item scene: root Node3D, origin at ground contact, faces -Z, `footprint` metadata.
`decoration_manager.gd` (`/root/World/Decorations`): restores placed items from GameState on planet load; `place(item_id, dir, yaw) -> instance_id`, `remove(instance_id)`.
`placement_controller.gd`: press `decorate` → opens inventory (decorations tab) → pick → ghost follows a point ~2.5 m in front of the player on the surface (uses `Planet.surface_point`), `rotate_left/right` rotates 15°, `interact` confirms, `cancel` exits; blocked spots tint red. Also "pick up" mode for placed items (walk up, `interact` on a placed item with a prompt "Pick up" — placed items are Interactables).

### Rocket
`rocket_pad.tscn`: a landing pad + parked rocket at `planet.pad_transform()`; Interactable prompt "Fly". On interact: player boards (walks in, door closes), liftoff animation (shake, smoke, flame), then `SceneRouter.go_to_space(planet_id)`.
`space_travel.tscn`: 3D "solar system" view: starfield skybox (shader), nebula, the 4 planets as miniature globes matching their biomes, orbit lines, rocket. Player picks a destination (`move_left/right` cycles, `interact` confirms, `cancel` returns to the origin planet), rocket flies there with a swooping camera (5–7 s), then `SceneRouter.go_to_planet(dest)` (spawn at pad, landing animation plays in rocket_pad on arrival when `GameState.previous_planet_id != ""`).

### Hub (hub planet, `buildings` = town_hall, deco_store, clothes_store, event_space)
Buildings are Node3D with `attach_to_planet(planet)` placing them at fixed dirs (define in the building script). Each has an Interactable door. Shops open `src/ui/shop/shop_panel.tscn` (UI builder: generic buy/sell grid panel with `open(items: Array, mode: "buy"|"sell", title: String)`). Clothes store: `clothing_catalog.gd` (suits/hats/backpacks with `style` dict) + preview mannequin. Town Hall: mayor NPC (elder robot "Mayor Orbit") — rename home planet, see stats, daily bulletin. ## 9. Time
`Environment` owns the clock: 1 in-game day = 10 real minutes (`DAY_LENGTH_SEC = 600`), stored in `GameState.time_of_day` (hours). Emits `EventBus.time_of_day_changed`. Phases: dawn 5–7, day 7–18, dusk 18–20, night 20–5. Night has stars, moon(s), shooting stars, glowing props, fireflies.
The sun and moons orbit in the player's local frame (`environment.gd` header), so the time of day is the same wherever you stand: day and night are a time, not yet a place (§1.1). Music follows the phase, not the real-world hour — `AudioManager` swaps a planet's `_day` / `_night` file at the phase change. The only real-world clock in the game is space trash's (§11).

### 9.1 `astro_night` — global shader parameter (ENVIRONMENT BUILDER owns it, everyone reads it)
`project.godot [shader_globals]` declares **`astro_night`** (`float`, default `1.0`). `src/world/environment.gd`
writes it every frame with `RenderingServer.global_shader_parameter_set(&"astro_night", night)`.

* **Semantics**: `0.0` = full daylight, `1.0` = full night. It is exactly `Environment.get_night_factor()`
  (the `EnvPalette.night` curve): 0 from 06:24 to 18:24, ramping to 1 by 20:24 and back down at dawn.
  It is a smooth float — never test it with `==`, ramp with it.
* **Default when no Environment exists** (isolated showcases, tests) is `1.0`, i.e. emissives glow at
  full strength, so a scene without the day/night system still looks right.
* **How to use it** in any `.gdshader`:
  ```glsl
  global uniform float astro_night;
  // ... lamps, screens, crystals: dim by day so they do not blow out at noon
  EMISSION = emission_color.rgb * emission_strength * mix(0.15, 1.0, astro_night);
  // ... fake speculars / painted highlights: fade OUT at night so they stop blooming
  hl *= mix(1.0, 0.2, astro_night);
  ```
* Reference implementations: `src/shaders/toon_soft.gdshader` (emission scaling + `emission_cap`,
  which rescales an emissive so its brightest channel stays under ~1.9 and the core keeps its hue
  instead of clipping to white) and `src/shaders/visor.gdshader` (highlight fade).
* Decoration / planet / hub builders: put emissive props on `MaterialLib.glow()` and you get this for
  free. Only add `global uniform float astro_night;` yourself if you write a bespoke shader.

## 11. Planet Score, Trust tiers & Space Trash
Added 2026-09-06. **Status: all three systems are UNDER REVIEW pending the core-loop decision
(§1.1).** The code is unchanged and everything below describes what ships; if your change extends
one of them, flag that in your report (§1.1).

Together they give the game a goal beyond "place things": a number for the home planet that rises
with what you build there (Planet Score), a reason for trust with Zorp and Bolt to keep paying off
(trust tiers), and light pressure to keep coming home (space trash).

**`PlanetScore`** (`src/planet/planet_score.gd`, no owner Node — pure static function of GameState):
`PlanetScore.compute(planet_id := "home") -> Dictionary` returns `{score: float 0-100, stars: int
1-5, decor_points, variety_points, trust_points, trash_penalty, trash_count}`. Score = rarity-weighted
decor points (capped 50) + category-variety bonus (capped 30, 5 per distinct category present) +
trust bonus (capped 10, average Zorp/Bolt friendship) - trash penalty (5 per piece of trash at home,
uncapped). `PlanetScore.star_glyphs(stars) -> String` for a quick "★★★☆☆" readout. Town Hall's
`_stats_flow()` is the only current caller; a HUD/UI badge is open for the UI builder to add.

**Trust tiers** (`src/favors/favor_system.gd`): `TRUST_PAL` (15) and `TRUST_BEST_FRIEND` (30) sit on
the same 0-100 friendship value `NpcData.tier_for()` already uses for greetings, but are a separate,
higher scale — greeting tiers are tuned to change dialogue quickly, these gate real rewards. Reaching
pal/best-friend gives +20%/+40% favor stardust (read from friendship *before* that favor's own
friendship bump, so the bonus reflects standing trust). Hitting best-friend once grants a one-time
signature decoration on top of the usual reward roll (`SIGNATURE_REWARD`: zorp -> `deco_wish_star`,
bolt -> `deco_robot_dog`), gated by `GameState.flags["signature_unlocked_<npc>"]` so it never repeats.

**Space trash** (`src/planet/trash_system.gd` + `trash_piece.gd`, PLANET BUILDER territory):
`GameState.trash_home` (Array of `{id, dir, kind}`, `dir` local to the home planet like a placed
decoration) and `GameState.trash_last_check_unix` (a Unix timestamp) are the persisted state.
`/root/World/TrashField` (`res://src/planet/trash_system.tscn`, spawned by world.gd like every other
optional piece) is a no-op on every planet except home. On `_ready`, when `current_planet_id ==
"home"`, it converts real seconds elapsed since `trash_last_check_unix` into new trash pieces (one
per `SECONDS_PER_TRASH` = 900s, capped at `MAX_TRASH` = 10), placed via `Planet.find_free_dir` exactly
like a favor's fetch targets, then resets the timestamp. There is no per-frame timer — trash only
ever appears to have piled up "since you last looked": it accrues in one step when home loads, never
while the player watches.
`TrashPiece` (`class_name TrashPiece`, extends `Interactable`, one of 3 procedural `kind`s: "can",
"scrap", "wrapper") is not a decoration — it is never in the inventory or catalog. Interacting with
one calls `GameState.remove_trash(id)`, pays 4-10 stardust, and frees itself.
`EventBus.trash_changed(count)` fires on every spawn and cleanup for UI to listen to.
`trash_last_check_unix` defaults to "now" (not 0) everywhere it can be missing — a fresh boot, an old
save with no key — specifically so it never reads as decades of elapsed time and dumps a maxed pile.
Regression test: `tests/trash_catchup_probe.tscn`.
