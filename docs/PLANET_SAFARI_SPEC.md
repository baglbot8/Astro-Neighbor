# Planet safari spec: photographing the neighbours' worlds

Status: started 2026-09-23 by the lead from the user's own words. Sections 0 and 1 are the user's
rulings; later sections are filled in from a read-only map of the code and a research pass before any
builder starts. Builders do not edit this file; the lead amends it with the measurement that
justifies a change.

## 0. How we got here

After the second rebuild of the flight safari, the user flew it, caught 5 of 17, and said:

> "I feel like the space picture safari still feels a bit lifeless. Most of the time you're just
> looking at empty space and the 'Reward' is seeing a couple small specks of light ... altogether it
> still feels more interesting to explore the planets and meet neighbors than it does to do the
> safari and try to perfect photos or snap rare sights."

The lead's diagnosis, from the code: the flight window is a flat 2D painting (a real 3D space scene is
built and hidden behind it the whole flight), each route's 8-10 named landmarks are drawn nowhere, and
most sights are small abstract lights that do not react to the player. The user then proposed three
kinds of safari — a reworked space safari (a real 3D window, big creatures that react, a "call"
button, one theme per route), a **neighbour planet safari**, and a multi-zone mystery planet on rails —
and chose to **try the neighbour planet safari first**:

> "Bring the safari to the planets we worked hard to design. So I'd go to Bolt's planet and go into
> safari mode, where I have 3 minutes in first person to snap photos of his planet's unique events or
> creatures. You can move freely and see their planet more up close. You can even take picture of
> your neighbors and keep them."

The space safari stays exactly as built (`merge4`) while this is tried. The mystery planet is later.

## 1. The user's rulings (2026-09-23)

1. **Free movement, not rails.**
   > "I think having the freedom would be great. Players can start to get used to the planets events
   > and know that if they want to get a picture of the seawhale in the sky, they need to run to the
   > other side of the planet at the start of the course. It encourages more replay and isn't the same
   > track over and over again. It also differentiates from pokemon snap in that way."

   So each planet's events happen at **learnable places and times** within the safari, and the skill
   is knowing where to be. First person, about **3 minutes**.
2. **Neighbours are photo subjects too.**
   > "Photos of neighbors can be a bonus log in the journal of photographing your friends and players
   > can choose to keep their favorite photos of their neighbors."
3. **One safari per day per planet.**
4. **Neighbours ask for planet photos.**
   > "maybe the neighbors ask you to take photos of their planet and achieve a certain total score for
   > all the photos, or want a specific semi-rare event, etc."

   The lead reads this as the direction for the story's photo asks (`STORY_SPINE_SPEC.md`): once the
   planet safari works, a neighbour's last request before the ship part can be about **their own
   world** — a total score across a safari's photos, or one specific semi-rare event — instead of a
   sight in the sky. "Maybe" — to be confirmed with the user once the first planet is playable.

5. **The life only happens in safari mode.**
   > "I think unless you're in safari mode the life and events on the planet dont happen."

   Outside a safari the planet is exactly today's planet. The creatures and events exist only while a
   safari runs. The lead's reading of what that buys: no extra cost on the phone in normal play, a
   self-contained system that is created when a safari starts and removed when it ends, and a safari
   that feels like the planet *waking up*.
6. **Crossing the planet is a commitment.**
   > "I dont want to be able to fully walk around the planet too fast. So going to one side of the
   > planet should feel like a bit of a commitment that you might miss an event happening in another
   > part of the planet. Instead of making planets larger, we can slightly slow down the character or
   > shrink the duration that certain events may last (e.g. maybe they only last 20 seconds)"

   Planets keep their size. Two levers instead: a **slower walk during a safari**, and **short event
   windows (about 20 s)**. The design rule the numbers must meet: **reaching the far side takes longer
   than an event lasts**, so choosing one side means risking an event on the other. The lead's reading,
   to confirm with the user: the slower walk applies **only in safari mode** — normal walking on every
   planet stays exactly as it is.

Carried over from the flight, because the user asked for them for all photos (2026-09-23): **after the
safari, a review of every photo**, one at a time, with its own scores and a short line — *centred
10/10, size 7/10, focus 8/10, a rarity boost* — adding up to what it is worth, and, when you already
have a photo of that subject, a choice to **keep the new one or the old one**.

## 2. The plan

**One planet first — Bolt's.** Prove that first-person photo play on a planet feels good before
building five. Then the other four, then the story asks move onto the planets.

## 3. What the code map found (2026-09-23, read-only Opus map of `merge4`)

* **Bolt's world is tiny.** Radius 10.5 m (`bolt.tres:9`), half circumference 33.0 m. Today's run is
  7.0 m/s and walk 4.2 m/s (`player.gd:9-13`): **the far side is 4.7 s away running, 7.9 s walking.**
  Three minutes of running is about 19 laps. The curve does hide the far side: a 1.4 m neighbour
  vanishes beyond 9.8 m, and from the pad nothing on the far ~65 degrees of Bolt can be seen.
* **There is no animal on any planet, and nothing on a schedule.** Bolt's world has Bolt (who wanders
  to a random point 1.4-9 m from home, `npc.gd:142`), spinning gear trees, blinking antennas, steam
  vents, six always-on lamps, and a ring in the sky — the only sky object fixed to a planet
  (`environment.gd:787-800`). Fireflies and shooting stars at night follow the player. **Every
  creature, event, schedule and place-to-be is new work.**
* **The sky has no fixed places.** Sun, moons, stars and the other worlds are laid out around the
  camera. A creature "over the far side" must be a new object placed in the world.
* **The camera is third person only** (`camera_rig.gd:3`): it looks at a point above the player and its
  pitch is a height angle, so it can never look up at the sky. First person needs a new branch.
  Every input path — mouse look, phone drag-to-look (`touch_controls.gd:701-731`), keys — already
  reaches the rig.
* **The astronaut cannot be hidden with `visible`**: `EventBus` forces physics, input and `visible`
  back on after 12 s (`event_bus.gd:151-175`).
* **Nothing can hide the HUD while the player keeps walking.** A modal hides everything and also
  freezes walking and looking. A photo mode needs one new gate that the HUD pills, the photo-journal
  button, the compass pips, the "!" markers, the mini-game pill and both J keys all read.
* **No photo in the game is a real picture.** Every photo is re-drawn by a shader from a few numbers,
  and no pixels are ever saved (`sky_journal.gd:466`). "See your photos" and "keep your favourite"
  need real frame capture — never done here, cost on the phone unmeasured.
* **The journal would lose planet photos on reload**: it deletes any id that is not a flight sight
  (`sky_journal.gd:481`) and builds its pages only from sky sights (`:232-246`).
* **Mini-games are the nearest pattern** (start, life cycle, the direction arrow), but their contract
  forbids taking the camera, changing input and timers (`minigame_system.gd:76-79`). The safari
  borrows their pieces and is its own system.
* **Three real minutes cost 4.26 game hours by day and 2.94 by night** on the normal clock.
* **Nothing in this project has ever been timed on a phone.** A character model costs about 58 draw
  calls (OPEN_ISSUES 61); a new shader stalls 90-480 ms the first time it draws unless warmed up
  (OPEN_ISSUES 55); GPU particles stalled every visit and CPU particles fixed it (OPEN_ISSUES 62).

## 4. What the research found (Alba, TOEM, Pupperazzi, Umurangi Generation, New Pokemon Snap)

Sourcing: developer interviews for Alba, TOEM and Pupperazzi; reviews and guides for the rest.

* **Warn before every event**, with a sight and a sound that duplicate each other so a muted phone
  loses nothing — that is what makes "run to the far side" a choice instead of luck.
* **Every photo game studied freezes walking while the camera is up.** No game found does live
  move + aim + zoom + shoot on a touchscreen; they all split *walk* from *shoot*.
* **Fixed, learnable timings** (Snap) — but on a small sphere players learn a whole schedule in one or
  two visits, so replay needs variety: day and night versions, and a rare event on some days.
* **Requests name the subject plainly**, and a satisfied request is confirmed at once with a chime.
* **The best moment of a review is a character's own reaction** to the photo, not the number.
* **None of the cozy photo games uses a hard countdown.** A daily cap is accepted; a ticking clock
  reads as a chore. So the three minutes are framed as **the planet waking up**, then going back to
  sleep — not a timer to beat.
* **A film limit relaxes players** more than a clock does (Shutter Stroll's 24 frames).
* Silhouette readable at a distance, full detail only up close.

## 5. The design (lead, 2026-09-23 — sections 5.1, 5.2 and 5.5 to be confirmed by the user)

### 5.1 Crossing the planet is a commitment — the numbers

Rule 6 made measurable: **from the moment an event's warning appears, a player standing on the far
side cannot arrive before it ends; a player a quarter of the way round can.** With an 8 s warning and
a 12-20 s event, the far side must take more than about 20 s and a quarter must take less.

On Bolt that means a **safari walk of about 1.5 m/s**: the far side takes 22 s, a quarter takes 11 s.
That is about a third of today's walk — not "slightly" slower — but in first person 1.5 m/s is an
ordinary walking pace; today's 4.2 m/s would feel like a jog at eye height. **No running and no
jetpack during a safari; a small hop is kept.** Normal play is untouched. The pace is one constant
per planet and will be tuned after the user plays it.

### 5.2 How a safari starts and ends

* **Bolt offers it.** Talk to him on his world: "Photo safari?" — once per day (`flags`, keyed by day,
  the way the replay board does it). After it: "The yard's asleep now. Come back tomorrow."
* **It always starts at the landing pad**, facing the same way, so "the far side at the start" means
  something. A short fade takes you there.
* **The planet wakes up for three minutes**, then everything goes back into hiding with a last puff of
  steam. No ticking countdown in your face — a small sun-dial in the corner shows how much is left.
* The world clock runs as normal (about 4 game hours by day). Day or night is fixed at the start and
  picks which version of the schedule runs.

### 5.3 First person, two modes

* **Walking:** today's stick on the left, drag to look on the right, a camera button.
* **Camera up:** walking stops. Drag to aim, pinch or a slider to zoom (45 degrees down to about 12),
  and a shutter. **Tap = the picture now; press and hold = the focus settles, release takes a sharper
  picture** — the same "tap, or hold for better" the flight taught.
* The astronaut is hidden by the camera's render mask, never by `visible` (the 12 s watchdog).
* A new photo-mode gate hides the HUD pills, journal button, compass pips, "!" markers and the
  mini-game pill, and gives the safari its own small layer: shutter, zoom, film left, the sun-dial.

### 5.4 A photo is a real picture

The shutter captures the actual frame. During the safari the pictures live in memory; only the ones
you keep are saved, as small thumbnails. The builder must **measure** the capture stall and the saved
size on the phone renderer; a shutter flash can cover a small hitch.

**Scoring, the four numbers the user asked for, each shown out of 10 in the review:**
* **Centred** — how far the subject's centre is from the frame's centre, measured on screen.
* **Size** — how much of the frame it fills, against a best band for that subject (too small or cut off
  both score lower). It gives zoom a real purpose.
* **Focus** — whether the focus had settled on the subject's distance when the picture was taken.
* **Rarity** — a boost by how rare the subject or moment is.

A subject hidden behind the planet or a prop (a ray from the camera) does not count. The numbers fold
into the flight's existing `SafariScoring` for grade and price.

### 5.5 Bolt's world wakes up — the list for the user to approve

Places, measured round the planet from the pad ("Base"): the Vent Field (a quarter round one way),
Gear Grove (a quarter the other way), Antenna Hill, and the Far Side.

### 5.6 After the safari: the review, and the journal

* **Every photo, one at a time**, with its four numbers as bars, what it is worth, and a line from Bolt
  about it. If you already have a photo of that subject: **keep the new one, or the old one**.
* **Friends** — a new journal section: your favourite photo of each neighbour.
* The journal is taught to keep planet subjects across a reload.

### 5.7 Not in this first build

The other four planets; moving the story's photo asks onto the planets (the user's "maybe"); the
review screen for the space flight; the space safari rework.

### 8.1 P5 is re-diagnosed, not looped

Round 2's critic pinned one cause: the card's content box inside the new ScrollContainer does not
expand sideways (700 px of content in a 1363 px view), so everything was squeezed into the left half,
the photos were shrunk to 260 px to compensate, and a two-line voice line pushed *Keep new / Keep old*

(P4's content), not a placeholder.

### 8.2 Rulings on what the critics raised

* **Camera up locks walking — full stop.** P3 made a stick push past half lower the camera. A thumb
  resting on the stick while aiming would drop it. Only the camera button lowers it.
* **Focus must not punish small creatures.** When the centre ray slips off a small moving subject the
  focus jumped to the sky and scored 0, and a 0 made any photo a Smudge. Keep the last lock when the
  ray misses; a tap focuses at once on what the centre ray hits; holding makes it better, not
  possible. A well-centred, well-sized photo of a nut-crab must be reachable.
* **Overlaps must force a choice** (6.2). P4's critic measured that both events of each pair could be
  reached in one round. Move and shorten them until they cannot — measured by walking, not asserted.
  P4's moved places (the geyser near the start, the snail from Bolt's home to the crates) are accepted.
* **The Great Magnet's red** measured S 0.65 median; bring it under the 0.60 gate.
* **The whale's spout rule** misses about 1 in 3 visible puffs and pays wrongly about 1 in 91. Accepted
  as strict-but-honest.
* **Photos pay.** The review said "worth N stardust" and nothing paid it. The review pays the session's
  total when it closes.
* **The secrets page lists every subject** of the planet — including ones that have never woken —
  as "????".
* **One question per talk.** On a first meeting Bolt asked for a favour and then offered a safari. The
  offer gives way to any favour or story beat in that talk.
* **The onboarding tip** ("See something shiny?...") showed during a safari. Hide tips while
  `PhotoMode.active`.
* **`environment.gd:508` floods "The axis Vector3 must be normalized"** every frame after a safari
  (43-61 errors per run, intermittent). Unknown cause: diagnose, then fix.
* **One 143 ms frame** at the fade-in/awake boundary: find whose it is; warm it up.
* **Integration traps from P1's critic:** `set_first_person(false)` resets look scale and zoom opt-in;
  `part_celebration.gd:577` copies the camera's cull mask; `dialogue_runner._push_in` tweens the rig's
  FOV. Check none bites during or after a safari.

> "Overall I think it felt pretty good - definitely more fun than the [space] safari. Mostly I think
> it was easy to get an almost perfect shot. Maybe we need to factor in direction (direction that the
> target is facing) as another factor. I never found the zoom, but never felt the need to use it
> especially since my score was high."
>
> "The speed is fine, but mostly the planet didn't have a lot going on so I was mostly wandering
> looking for anything happening. I didn't really pay attention to the hints though because I felt
> like it was more fun to wander around instead of just following the instructions. That said, maybe

> time and common events happen more than once and stay for a moderate amount of time?"
>
> "I kept choosing photos that were worse than my old photos, because I would only find out the score
> after. I almost didn't mind though because it made me have to look at each photo closely and decide
> which I think would score higher. I could see some people not liking this though."

What it tells the lead:
* **The planet safari is the right direction.** It beat the space safari in the user's own words.
* **Scoring is too lenient**: a near-perfect shot takes no skill, so zoom never mattered. Add
  **facing** (which way the subject looks) and make **size** demand getting close or zooming.
* **The player wanders; they do not follow hints.** Design for the wanderer: the planet must be dense
  enough that wandering bumps into things. Warnings stay, but nothing may depend on reading them.
* **The user's frequency tiers** become the schedule rule: rare = once; uncommon = several times, short;
  common = several times, a moderate while.
* **Walking pace is right.** Keep 1.5 m/s.
* **Keep-new-or-old is blind** and the user was mixed; a decision for the user.

### 11.1 Scoring gets a skill ceiling

* **FACING**, a new score out of 10 — which way the subject looks relative to the camera. **API, fixed
  by the lead so content and scoring can be built in parallel:** a registered subject may carry
  `"front": Callable` returning the world-space unit vector its face points along. The score is
  `(dot(front, dir_subject_to_camera) + 1) * 5` — facing you 10, side-on 5, its back 0. A subject with
  no `front` (a shower, a geyser) has no Facing score and the bar is not shown.
* **SIZE demands getting close or zooming.** Today a crab 3-5 m away scored 10 at 45 degrees. The
  scorer's rule (R3) falls off smoothly outside a subject's best band — no flat 10 across a wide band;
  each subject's band (R4) is set so that **at 45 degrees and its usual distance (3 m or more for a
  creature) size scores 6 or less, and zoomed or close enough to fill its band it scores 10.**
* **Calibration gate**, on the merged build: a *careless* test player (points roughly, never zooms, any

### 11.2b Not too common either — the density BAND (user ruling, 2026-09-24, before bed)

  different subjects on screen at once for no more than 10% of the safari**.
