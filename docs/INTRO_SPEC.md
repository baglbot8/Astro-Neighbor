# Intro spec: the cockpit opening and the first hour

Status: **APPROVED by the user and BUILT, 2026-10-18. Not yet played by the user.** Section 11 records
what was built and the rulings made while building; where section 11 and an earlier section disagree,
section 11 wins. Builders do not edit this file; the lead amends it with the measurement that justifies
a change.

This file **wins over `STORY_HOME_SPEC.md` beats 1-2 and sections 5.1-5.2, and over `CORE_LOOP.md`,
on the opening and the first hour** (new game to the first neighbour world). Everything after the
first neighbour trip stays as `STORY_HOME_SPEC.md` and `STORY_SPINE_SPEC.md` say.

## 0. Why

The crash alone does not say who you are, why you fly, or what you stand to lose. The finale needs
four things planted early: you take photos, a meteor is coming, the neighbours are packing, and home
is worth keeping (that last one stays tiny; the neighbours' choice to stay is the finale's surprise).
The opening should also read as a mature game's cold open: quiet, a little funny, short on talk.

## 1. User rulings (2026-10-18)

1. **First-person cockpit.** You sit in the ship, cruising through space. You turn your head only; you
   cannot walk around. The Professor's radio plays the whole time.
2. **Things to look at,** each with one short line. The user's examples: a photo of your parents, the
   job at the Intergalactic Station ("photos of mineral samples"). The user then merged the parents
   into the photo wall: the parents' photo is **seen on the wall, with no line of its own**.
3. **When all are seen, the giant meteor comes into view** while you were looking elsewhere. The ship
   turns and grazes it. You are flung toward the small planet that becomes home. The Professor talks
   you through it.
4. **Over the radio** the Professor asks your name and if you are hurt, says the ship will not fly far
   and needs help, says the neighbours are busy leaving, and asks you to meet him on the Commons.
5. **At the start only the Commons can be reached** (plus home). He gives you favours there. They end
   in your first repair, which lets you fly to your first neighbours. He asks you to deliver
   something to Zorp.
6. **Why you photograph their worlds: option A, "one last look".** The Professor learns you are a
   photographer. Residents can help themselves by checking nothing is left behind, and your photos
   can show what they might miss. Helping them gets you parts.
7. **Name: pick from a list.** Not typed.
8. **Cover the camera change with the impact flash.** The cockpit cuts to the exterior fall under the
   flash. The old crash shot's no-cut promise is lifted for this one cut.
9. The cockpit lines in section 4 were approved with the user's wording change to the job line.

## 2. Lead defaults (the user did not object; open to change)

* **No deadline.** "Make it to your first day in time" is flavour only. Nothing counts down. The
  standing rule in `STORY_HOME_SPEC.md` 2.2 is no clock or countdown anywhere.
* **The Commons repair is a "patch", not a sixth ship part.** Departure Day still comes when part
  five fits. The patch is not in `CampaignData.PARTS` and does not count in `rocket_part_count`.
* **The rocket stays rusty** after the patch. Finish stages are unchanged (`CampaignData.finish_stage`).
* **Text layout:** radio lines at the top, item lines at the bottom, never typing together. The radio
  waits while you read an item line.
* **Look control:** drag to look (finger or mouse). A small dot at screen centre; hold it on an item
  for about 0.5 s and its line appears. No tap needed.
* **Never stuck:** if the player has not seen every item at 30 s of idle the Professor nudges. At 75 s
  the meteor comes anyway. Both numbers are guesses to tune from play.
* **The meteor you graze is the meteor of the story.** It is huge and slow, at the system's edge. Later
  it is the faint streak in every sky (`STORY_HOME_SPEC.md` 2.6). It is called "the meteor" in every line.

## 3. The opening, beat by beat

| # | Beat | Who drives | Rough length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fade in on the cockpit. Radio hums. | cinematic | 2 s |
| 2 | Look around. Four things. Radio chatter between. | **player** | 20-75 s |
| 3 | The meteor slides into the window. Alarm. "Look up!" | cinematic | 4 s |
| 4 | The ship swerves and grazes it. Shake, sparks, alarms. | cinematic | 3 s |
| 5 | White impact flash. **Cut** to the exterior. | cinematic | 0.3 s |
| 6 | The exterior fall, burn, landing, bounce, hatch (the shot already built). | cinematic | about 13 s |
| 7 | Control returns. The radio call: hurt?, name, ship, meteor, packing, meet me. | **player** | 40 s |

| 9 | Fly to Zorp with the parcel. The first neighbour. | **player** | |

Skip: one shared pop-up (`SkipConfirm.ask`, "Keep watching" focused) at any point from beat 1 to 6.
A confirmed skip ends in the same state as playing it all: the rocket on the pad, finish rusty, the
player standing, the same flags set. See `crash_intro.gd` `_skip` for the pattern.

## 4. The cockpit

**View.** First person, seated, in a small bright cockpit. The window shows deep space with the
little system ahead (the home planet and neighbour worlds hanging in the sky). Procedural art only,
inside the palette gates of `STYLE_GUIDE.md`. Gloved hands rest on the dash. Camera is fixed at the
seat and only turns: yaw about 75 degrees each way, pitch about 35 degrees each way (guesses to tune).

**The four things to look at,** each shows its line once. Revisiting shows nothing new.

| Thing | Where | Line (each box is at most 60 characters) |
|---|---|---|
| The photo wall | one side, string and pins, 5-6 small photos; **the parents' photo is one of them** | "Your photo wall. It has room for more." |
| Your camera | on the dash, brass, dented | "Your camera. Brass, dented and all yours." |
| The job letter | clipped by the window | "Almost day one of the new job at the Intergalactic Station!" then "If only it wasn't just photographing mineral samples..." |
| A tiny plant | stuck to the dash | "A tiny plant. You make every place homey." |

The wall plants the scrapbook (its last page is the finale's group photo). The plant plants
decorating. The camera plants the safari. The letter plants why you are out here.

Seen-state: a thing counts as seen when its line has finished. All four seen, and the radio line in
progress has finished, then a 2-second beat, then the meteor.

**Hint that things are interactive:** a soft pulse on each unseen thing, fading once seen.

**Radio during the cruise** (the Professor; draft lines, all under 60 characters):

* "Survey ship, this is Professor Comet. Loud and clear!"
* "You're passing through our little neighbourhood."
* "Quiet day. Just how I like the sky."
* Nudge: "Still there, pilot? Take a good look around."
* Late warning: "That rock is far bigger than my scope said!"
* Alarm: "Pilot! Look up! LOOK UP!"

The existing crash captions (`crash_intro.gd` `CAPTIONS`) cover the Professor's talk during the fall.
The builder reads that table and keeps only the lines that fit after the hit.

## 5. The hand-off to the crash shot

* `crash_intro.gd` has no start-time parameter today. It needs `start_t` (begin at the hit, about
  `HIT_T` 7.0) and a way to skip its navy card. Everything in it is a function of `_t`, so seeding
  `_t`, the one-shot flags (`cruise_loop`) and the caption index should be enough. It still forces
  the clean finish at `_begin` and steps it to rusty at the hit.
* The cockpit plays inside the live World, as a child of `IntroDirector` like `CrashIntro` is, so
  the rocket, planet, camera rig and player already exist. `environment.gd` locks the sky bearings
  from whatever camera is current in the first 1.5 s (`BODY_AZ_LOCK_SEC`). The gameplay rig must be
  current during that window, or the builder calls `reset_bearing_lock()` (`environment.gd:376`).
  The builder tries the simplest layout first, a cockpit node far from the world with its own
  camera, and falls back to a separate viewport only if the sky lock or the per-frame environment
  write breaks it. **Measure which one holds; do not reason it out** (`CLAUDE.md` evidence rules).
* The small lumpy rock in the old exterior shot must not look like the giant meteor. Lead default:
  hide it after the cut. Optional: a huge dark shape far behind the tumbling ship. The critic
  decides from frames.
* Set the same intro flags (`FLAG_CRASH` and the rest in `intro_director.gd`) at the same moments
  as today, so the opening never replays on a reload.

## 7. The first hour on the Commons

1. **The gate.** While `CampaignData.gates_on()` and the flag `first_patch` is unset, Zorp and Bolt
   are closed. Only home and the Commons (`hub`) are open. The gate lives in
   `CampaignData.planet_in_range` (`campaign_data.gd`), which seven other files call
   (`pad_destination_picker.gd:496`, `favor_system.gd:910`, `stamp_system.gd:117`, `stamp_card.gd`,
   `replay_board.gd:462`, `norm_system.gd:221`), so they inherit it. The picker shows only "Needs N
   parts" today (`pad_destination_picker.gd:497`); it gets a new closed-tile text for the patch.
   Old saves have gates off and stay fully open. The flag is a `GameState.flags` entry, no schema change.
2. **The meeting.** On the Commons the Professor greets you (the section 5.2 lines of
   `STORY_HOME_SPEC.md`: mind the boxes, everyone flies out together once your ship is fixed, not
   him). Then he sends you to three Commons residents.
3. **Three small errands.** Lead default: Pip, Pop and Stella each hold one bit of the patch. Each
   is one short talk and a small task, no flight. You carry the bits back. The Professor fits the
   patch (a short celebration; the rocket stays rusty). Total about 10-15 minutes, a first guess
   that a real play-through times. The three errands are a small scripted chain in a **new file**,
   not a `ProjectSystem` project: that system only loads the five neighbours (`project_system.gd:388-398`)
   and has no deliver step. State lives in `GameState.flags`, like the Professor's photo ask.
4. **The photographer line,** said when the patch is fitted (option A, drafts under 60 characters):
   * "A photographer? Splendid!"
   * "Nobody can see all of their own world."
   * "Your photos could show what they might leave behind."
   * "Help them, and they will help fix your ship."
   Then the Professor's photo ask opens (`ProfessorAsk.open_task()`), reworded so the reason is the
   residents' last look, not "for my records".
5. **The parcel.** "First, a parcel for Zorp. His rivers are dimming." Lead default: spare glow
   bulbs. Delivering it is Zorp's first talk, which starts his project as it does today
   (`project_system.gd:874`). `favor_system.gd` has a "deliver" type (`:22`, `:198`) and
   `conversation.gd` `_receive_gift` handles it; the builder picks the lighter of those and a flag.
6. The neighbours' "one last photo of home" step and the finale's callbacks in
   `STORY_HOME_SPEC.md` are unchanged. This section only gives the Commons a first-hour shape.

## 9. Gates (the smallest set that shows it works)

## 11. What was built, and what was ruled while building (2026-10-18)

Three Opus builders, one critic each, a round 2 for each, then an integration run. Files: section 8, with
two corrections: `conversation.gd` lives in `src/dialogue/`, and B3 also edited `src/favors/favor_system.gd`
(`_templates_for` only). New test timelines: `tests/director/flow_*.json`, `cockpit_idle.json`, and
`flow_full_real.json` (the real cockpit, then the crash, the radio call and the name picker; the
`flow_opening.json` timeline was written for a 3 s stub and only opens skip pop-ups with the real cockpit).

**Cockpit (B1).** A cream and teal crochet cabin; four targets as specced (photo board with the parents'
photo, brass camera, job letter, sprout). Rulings made on the critic's findings:
* **Taps do nothing.** A tap on a target used to open "Skip the intro?" on a phone. Now a small "Skip"
  button (about 44 pt, bottom right) is the only pointer way to skip; keys, pad and the skip actions still work.
  A drag never skips. RadioCaption's "Tap to skip" pill is hidden while the cockpit plays.
* **The meteor is giant:** radius 36 m (about 41 m with squash, home is about 16 m), a dark grey-brown rock with
  a rim light, entering below the radio box; orange streak sparks at the graze. It was 11 m and read as one more
  rock in the first round.
* **The game clock is stopped** while the cockpit plays and restored at the cut, so the hand-back reads about
  09:49 (the old opening read about 10:01).
* **No nudge** when the forced meteor is under 8 s away.
* **Palette ruling:** for the cabin the binding gates are saturation p90 <= 0.68, blown < 5%, luma p95 <= 0.93
  and no dominant swatch above S 0.60. The saturation-mean, value-mean, luma-p05 and range bands were calibrated
  on planet daytime frames and are only advisory for a pale cabin. The window's navy space is exempt.
* **Not done:** no alarm sound exists (it reuses the "blocked" buzz, rocket_land and finale_impact); the user
  should listen. The lamp light was not baked (it changed the room's brightness 0.70 to 0.63).

**Flow (B2).** `IntroDirector` creates a waiting `CrashIntro` on frame 1 (astronaut hidden and frozen, rig
snapped, a "cutscene" modal held), then the cockpit. The cockpit's `finished(false)` calls `go()`; skip calls
`finish_skipped()`. The cut is covered by the white flash. Radio call order: "Pilot? Come in, pilot! Can you hear
me?", "What a tumble!", hurt, name picker, welcome with the name, ship hurt and one hop to the Commons, the two
5.1 meteor boxes, everyone busy getting ready to go, meet me on the Commons, then the kept control tutorial.
`ProfessorAsk.open_task()` is no longer called there. The picker is a 4x2 grid (Astro, Juno, Pebble, Mochi, Skye,
Pixel, Sprout, Ember) and writes `GameState.player_style["name"]`. Day-1 letter line 4 reads "Your rocket can
still make one hop: to the Commons." Quitting during the cockpit recovers to a playable game (the opening is
simply skipped on relaunch); this window is now 20-75 s instead of 22 s.

**First hour (B3).** `CampaignData.PATCH_GATED = ["zorp","bolt"]` and `needs_patch()`; the picker shows
"Needs a patch". `FirstHour.patch_fitted()` is the flag `first_patch` OR a heuristic that keeps a campaign save from
before this feature open if it already reached Zorp or Bolt. Chain: Pip (pick up 4 scrap), Pop (find his brass
bit on the grass between his shop and the stage), Stella (lend her tape, walk within 5 m of the rocket pad to
measure the dent). The fitting is a dialogue, a chime, two emotes and a toast, not the part celebration. Then the
photographer lines, the photo task opens, and the parcel (spare glow bulbs) is delivered by talking to Zorp, whose
normal project then starts in the same talk. The patch leaves `rocket_part_count()` at 0 and the finish rusty.
While the gate is closed Commons residents only offer deliveries (a "bring" favour could ask for a flower from a
closed world). **Changed in `STORY_HOME_SPEC.md` 5.2:** the Professor's thanks no longer says "I'm tracking that
meteor. Every sky you photograph helps."; the reason is the residents' last look at home. (Correction 2026-10-18: the
streak was removed from the sky on 2026-09-28, so the `town_hall` line "That streak is in all your photos" is wrong and is
replaced in `docs/STORY_FIT_REVIEW.md` A4.)

**Known and unproven.**
* No real finger was used anywhere. Drag-look and hold-the-dot on the iPhone are the user's test (gate 9).
* Phone cost and heat of the cockpit are unmeasured (CPU render 1.4-1.7 ms on the desktop, GPU time unreadable
  in Compatibility; the lamp adds an extra full-screen yarn pass). Watch for heat on the first phone play.
* The chain is estimated at 4-6 minutes (a model, not a timed play; section 7 guessed 10-15). The Professor's
  first meeting is 12 boxes and the fitting 13. The user's play decides whether to lengthen or split them.
* Pop's bit is small from the stage front (visible from Pop's shop); real players decide if it is findable.
* `docs/OPEN_ISSUES.md` 73 lists the traps found.

**Round 3, after the user's first look (2026-10-18).** The user: "the crochet of the interior cockpit is very hard to look at
with all the crochet inside it, can we make it like panels or something to reduce the intensity of the crochet? also it feels
very rectangular, can we add some rounded corners to the window?" Changed in `cockpit_props.gd` only: the walls, ceiling, front
plate, bezel, dash body, teal band, knobs, radar, camera body and sleeves are smooth matte toon panels (cream, seams, rivets);
crochet stays only on the mittens and cuffs, the dash-edge cushion, the camera strap, the plant and pot, the photo board's border
and the seat (measured 0-6% of the screen per view). The window is a rounded rectangle with radius 0.30 m (35% of its 0.86 m
height) and a rolled bezel; the letter sits 4 cm further out. Palette rulings unchanged: the navy space through the window pushes
the whole-frame saturation p90 to 0.66-0.71 (the interior crops are 0.17-0.42); space is exempt. Blown is 0.0-0.1% and luma p95
is at most 0.919. Not measured: the phone-speed gain from dropping the full-screen yarn (GPU time reads 0 on this Mac).
