# Next two worlds — build spec (not yet built)

Produced by a 13-agent design pass on 2026-09-07 and left here so the build does not have to
re-derive it. Three independent proposals were scored, one critic hunted for what would still read
as a reskin, and the judge MEASURED every proposed terrain config in headless Godot against the real
`DecorationManager` before choosing. The scores were:

* **atmosphere** — distinctiveness 9, feasibility 6, fits-art-direction 9
* **geometry** — distinctiveness 8, feasibility 6, fits-art-direction 9
* **ecology** — distinctiveness 7, feasibility 4, fits-art-direction 6

Winner: **atmosphere**.

## The synthesis (this is the build order)

BUILD: **Fen** (from `atmosphere`) + **Grig** (from `geometry`), with **Fen the creature** rebuilt on `geometry`'s eye engineering. Cut Squill, Thrum, Umbo and Tolla as worlds.

WHY THIS PAIR AND NOT THE OTHERS — MEASURED, NOT ARGUED. I ran all six proposed terrain configs through the real `DecorationManager` in headless Godot (4000 Fibonacci samples, fp 0.6; a synthetic home with no NPCs/buildings measures 75.8% free as the baseline):

    home_ref  free 75.8%   relief -0.93..0.59  water  1.5%  craters 3
    A_fen     free 72.3%   relief -1.04..0.15  water  5.6%  craters 14/14   <- BUILD
    G_grig    free 65.9%   relief -0.56..0.56  water  0.0%  5 shelves       <- BUILD
    E_tolla   free 75.7%   relief -1.07..0.15  water  0.0%  craters 12/12
    A_thrum   free 61.4%   water 0.6%, SHORE refusal 20.5%   (at -0.50: 81.0% free, 0.0% water)
    E_umbo    free 47.8%   water 0.5%, SHORE refusal 23.7%   (dry -99: 68.4% free)
    G_squill  free 45.7%   water 26.5%, shore 6.7%   (at its own fix -0.44: 46.4%, shore 26.1%)

THE FINDING THAT DECIDED IT: `SHORE_MARGIN` (0.18 m) punishes any world whose waterline sits on a broad flat shelf. Squill, Thrum and Umbo all put water on shelves and all three either drown the placement budget or produce no water at all. Squill's own named single knob (water_level -0.34 -> -0.44) does not rescue it — it converts 22 points of water-refusal into 19 points of shore-refusal and lands at 46.4%. Fen is the only water design that works, and it works because its water is in STEEP crater bowls: 5.6% coverage for only 2.5% shore refusal. Record this in `docs/OPEN_ISSUES.md`: **water goes in bowls, never on shelves.**

3. THE ONE ENGINE FIX BOTH WORLDS SHARE — terrace banks. Add `@export var terrace_bank: float = 0.0` to PlanetData (geometry's float strength, not atmosphere's bool — a strength is more useful and the 0.0 default leaves all four shipped worlds byte-identical). Implement it with **atmosphere's** bell, because it matches the crater/plateau house style already in the file:

        func _terrace_bank(d: Vector3) -> float:
            if data.terrace_bank <= 0.001: return 0.0
            var step := data.terrace_step * _vscale
            if step <= 0.001: return 0.0
            var f := (data.hill_amplitude * _vscale * _noise_hill.get_noise_3dv(d * radius)) / step
            var w := clampf(data.terrace_band, 0.02, 0.5)
            var t := smoothstep(0.5 - w, 0.5 + w, f - floorf(f))
            return clampf(data.terrace_bank * 4.0 * t * (1.0 - t), 0.0, 1.0)

    `bw = maxf(bw, _terrace_bank(d))` in `bank_weight()` AND `conc = maxf(conc, _terrace_bank(d) * 0.8)` in `_bake_color()` (atmosphere's extra step — each riser gets a contact-shade foot; geometry omits this). I measured the coverage: 6-9% of Grig's surface at bank > 0.5. That is a strong read without swamping the world, and `grass_planet.gdshader:201` already paints it with `bank_color` and already suppresses it inside the sand band. No shader edit. Set `terrace_bank = 1.0` on Grig. Without this Grig is a smooth pale dome — this is the change the whole world depends on.

CUT, AND WHY.
* **Squill's Shallows (geometry)** — measured 45.7% free, and its own named fix lands at 46.4% with shore refusal at 26.1%. A wading-shelf world is structurally incompatible with `SHORE_MARGIN` on broad flat ground. This is the same trap planet.gd's own `RIVER_MASK_LO` comment records from Zorp's rescue. Not tunable without abandoning the wading read.
* **Thrum's flooded terraces (atmosphere)** — measured 0.6% water at -0.30 with 20.5% shore refusal, and 0.0% water at -0.50. The flooded-shelf world does not exist in this engine.
* **Saphra, the 36-degree gas giant with a fill light peaking at local midnight** — this is the single strongest idea in all three proposals and it is being **deferred, not rejected**. It has no home in this pair (it would fight Fen's huge amber sun and swamp Grig's tiny-and-far read), and it carries the most new engine surface: a new node at a new `COMPANION_DISTANCE`, a third `DirectionalLight3D`, eight new PlanetData fields and slot-skipping logic. Make it world #7's headline.
* **Umbo (ecology)** — its six warm risers do not render (crater_count 0 + plateau_count 0 means `bank_weight()` returns zero everywhere and `bank_color` paints nothing), its `bracket_shelf` placement rule finds no valid directions, it measured 47.8% free, and its world-wide emissive mint carpet plus an omni under every cap pushes back toward the brightness R2.6 names.
* **Ecology's footstep-reactive shader block** — a per-fragment loop with an `acos` per step over six uniforms, added to `grass_planet.gdshader`, for a mechanic on a world we are not building. Any edit there can render the whole game unshaded white.
* **Tolla** — genuinely good and the best-measured config of all six (75.7%), but it is a flat pan with craters, which is the same landform family as Fen. Two flat cratered pans is the reskin failure the brief asks us to avoid. Grig is Fen's structural opposite. Hold Tolla's chime flats as the strongest candidate for world #8.

## Source specs for the two chosen worlds

### PLANET fen — Fen's Long Dusk (from the `atmosphere` proposal)

**Vibe.** The sun has been about to set for nine years, my shadow is seven metres long, and every pool on this pan is a mirror pointing straight back at it.

**What makes it not a reskin.** THE LIGHT: sun_peak_elev_deg = 11.0 instead of the hardcoded 52.0, so even at noon the sun is a hand's width off the horizon and EVERY shadow is 5.1x its caster all day. A 1.4 m astronaut throws a 7.2 m shadow; a 2.4 m standing stone throws 12.4 m. Nothing else in the game has ever moved the sun arc. Plus sun_disc_size 0.082 (3.2x the never-set shader default of 0.026) for a huge amber disc, moon_count = 0 (nobody has used 0 — the moonless world), cloud_density 0.9 (wisp_amount 0.144, nearly double the current maximum) so the low sun rakes through real dust filaments. THE TERRAIN: the only genuinely FLAT world — hill_amplitude 0.035 against the shipped 0.135-0.37 cluster, terrace_step 0.0 (terracing DISABLED, a first), plateau_count 0 (a first), hill_frequency 0.42 (below the 0.70-0.95 cluster: one hemisphere-scale swell, not landforms). And crater_count 14 against 3 on all four shipped worlds — the craters are not craters, they are the pools. Flat + long shadows is the whole idea: on a bumpy world a 7 m shadow is chopped up by relief and reads as nothing; on a pan it reads as a stripe across a third of the visible world.

**Terrain.** radius = 13.0 (unused; 10.5/12/21 taken). _vscale = 0.8125, _ascale = 0.6602. seed = 61. biome = "flats". uid://cplanetdatafen00.
