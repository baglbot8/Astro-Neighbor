# Story options: retelling Astro Neighbor around photography

Status: **PICKED 2026-09-26: Three, "Don't Move Out", with one user change.** The contract is now
`docs/STORY_HOME_SPEC.md`; this file is the record of the options.

> The user's change: the neighbours do **not** show they want to stay after you finish their mission.
> "That should be the bigger surprise in the end." At the hand-over they thank you, give the part, and
> wish you luck finding a new home, with at most a very slight hint that they love it, e.g. "ah I
> always loved sitting by that tree...I'll miss that", "I'll cherish this photo forever", "wow I
> forgot how nice of a view i have here".

Round 2 was drafted by the lead on 2026-09-26 from the user's four directions.

Round 1 (A "The Album", B "Postcards Home", C "The Waking Worlds") was declined: the user "didn't like
any of them in particular" and gave directions instead.

## User rulings (2026-09-26)

* **No story reason is needed for why planet life only happens in safari mode.** Players will accept
  it. This drops round-1 C's premise.
* The comet/meteor stays as the threat. Photos are how it is found, or fought, or both.
* The user's four directions, in short:
  1. **Research.** Your photos help the Professor. At the end he learns the worlds are lively
     because they sense danger: the meteor. You arrive because he called you, not by a crash. The
     user's own worry: then why do far planets "unlock" instead of you just flying there?
  2. **Wandering photographer.** You crash, sell photos to fix your ship, and make friends. Your
     photos find the meteor by chance. Friendship makes you want to save the creatures, even though
     the people could just leave.
  3. **Mission.** The meteor is known from the start and everyone is moving out. You show them the
     beauty of their homes. They fall back in love with them and help stop the meteor.
  4. **2 + 3.** You crash by chance, then find the system emptying. You only want to sell photos,
     fix your ship and leave too. Friendship turns everyone around, and all the ships together stop
     the meteor.

The three options below are directions 1, 2 and 4 (4 already contains 3), fleshed out.

## What must stay in every option (built, and the user likes it)

* Five neighbours (Zorp, Bolt, Fen, Grig, Vela), each with favours and a mini-game chain, each chain
  ending in a photo request on their own world, then the thing they give you
  (`STORY_SPINE_SPEC.md`, the user's ruling).
* The Professor's first task: photograph a neighbour and bring it to him on the Commons
  (`PLANET_SAFARI_SPEC.md` §15, being built now).
* Planet safaris, the scrapbook, Gloop buying prints.
* The finale: everyone gathers on the Commons (`PHASE5_SPEC.md`).

A clue all three options can share: **the creatures in your photos all face the same point in the
sky.** The game already scores which way a subject faces (Facing, `PLANET_SAFARI_SPEC.md` §11), so
the story can point at something the player really did. The Professor lays your album out and sees
it; that point is where the meteor will come from.

---

## One: "Field Notes" (the research story)

**Pitch.** You are Professor Comet's new field photographer. The little worlds have gone strangely
lively, and he needs pictures to find out why.

**Start.** No crash. The Professor's radio call is a job offer: "Something is stirring out there. I
need eyes and a good lens." You arrive in his old research hopper (the rusty rocket, now a loan) and
make a rough landing on the home planet.

**Why worlds open one by one** (the user's worry). The neighbours are private folk. Nobody lets a
stranger walk around their home with a camera. Favours and mini-games are how you earn their trust,
and at the end of each chain the neighbour gives you **an invitation**: their landing beacon. No
beacon, no landing. The unlock order becomes a friendship order.

**Middle.** The Professor sends research asks ("three Fine photos of Fen's pool creatures"). Your best
shots go up on a research wall on the Commons, which fills as you play.

**Turn.** The Professor lays out every photo: the creatures all face one point in the sky. The worlds
are lively because they sense something coming. A meteor.

**End.** From your album, the Professor works out the meteor's path. The neighbours fly their ships
out together and nudge it aside; it breaks into a harmless meteor shower. Everyone watches from the
Commons. Your last photo closes the Professor's notebook.

**What changes.** Biggest change. The intro's meaning (arrival, not crash), ship parts become
invitations, and "fix the ship" goes away as the goal.

**Why pick it.** Photos matter from the first minute, and the ending pays off every photo you took.

**Watch out.** It loses the stranded feeling the crash intro gives. Working for someone can feel like
a job, not an adventure.

## Two: "Snapshots for Scrap" (the stranded photographer)

**Pitch.** You crash-land, broke, with only a camera. Selling photos is how you get home. Friends are
what you find by accident.

**Start.** The crash intro as built. The Professor hears the crash on his radio: "Parts cost money,
friend, and folks here pay for good pictures."

**Middle.** You sell prints to Gloop for stardust. Neighbours help with ship parts in return for
favours; their last favour is always a photo of their world. Over time you go back to each world
because you like the people, not for the money. The Neighbours pages of the scrapbook become your
friends list.

**Turn.** Late, and by chance. The Professor pins your prints on his wall to admire them and sees it:
the creatures in every photo face the same spot in the sky. A meteor is coming. The people have ships
and could fly away. The creatures cannot.

**End.** Your ship is fixed. You could leave. You stay, and every neighbour's ship flies out with
yours to nudge the meteor aside. It breaks into a meteor shower, and the finale on the Commons
watches it together.

**What changes.** Smallest change. Keeps the crash, parts and prints. Adds the Professor's discovery
scene and a new reason for the finale.

**Why pick it.** Cheapest, and closest to what is built.

**Watch out.** The meteor arrives late and out of nowhere. For most of the game, the only big goal is
"fix the ship".

---

## Three: "Don't Move Out" (stranded, in a system that is packing up)

**Pitch.** You crash into a little solar system that is packing up to leave. You want to leave too.
Your photos change everyone's mind, yours included.

**Start.** The crash intro as built. The Professor's radio call: "Bad timing, friend. A meteor is
coming, and most folks are leaving." The first task sends you to him on the Commons, and there you see
it: moving boxes, "Gone to a bigger star" signs, a half-empty square. The Professor is the only one not
packing. He thinks the meteor can be stopped, but only with every ship, and nobody will listen.

**Middle.** Each neighbour is getting ready to go. Their favours and mini-games are packing-up jobs.
Their last request: **"One last photo of my home, before I go."** You take it on their world's safari.
They look at the photo and see how alive their home is. They unpack, give you the ship part, and
promise their ship to the Professor. Each neighbour you win back shows on the Commons: boxes gone,
lights back on.

**Turn.** With your album on his wall, the Professor sees that the creatures all face one point in the
sky. Now he knows where to push.

**End.** The ships everyone packed to escape in become the rescue fleet. Together they nudge the
meteor, and it breaks into a harmless meteor shower. Everyone watches from the Commons. The last photo
is the whole neighbourhood under the shower. Your ship is fixed too, but this is home now.

**What changes.** Medium. Keeps the crash, the parts, and the photo request as the last step. Changes
the Professor's radio lines, the flavour of each neighbour's chain (packing up), adds "emptying and
refilling" set dressing on the Commons, and a fleet scene for the finale.

**Why pick it.** Every neighbour's photo request gets a real reason, and it fits the built structure
exactly: photo, then they stay, then the part. It is the user's own combination.

**Watch out.** "Everyone is leaving" can feel sad in a cozy game. Keep it light (silly moving signs)
and add no countdown: the meteor comes when the story is ready, never on a clock.

---

## The lead's pick

**Three**, with the shared clue (the creatures all face the meteor). It keeps everything built, it
gives each photo request the strongest reason, and the ending turns everyone's getaway ships into the
fleet that saves home.

Two is the cheap fallback. One only if the user wants to drop the crash.

Possible later, not planned: the switched-off space safari flight code could come back once, for the
fleet flight in the finale.
