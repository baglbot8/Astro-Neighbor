# Safari flight spec: the rebuild after the first real play

Status: the contract for the flight rebuild, written 2026-09-21 by the lead from the user's own
words after **the first time a human being played the safari**. Builders do not edit this file; the
lead amends it with the measurement that justifies a change.

Everything before this document was measured by agents against synthetic input. The first real
player caught **nothing at all** on two flights. This spec exists because that happened.

---

## 0. What the player actually reported (2026-09-21, verbatim intent)

> "I played twice and didn't know how to take pictures, but also I didn't see anything to take a
> photo of. The sky was empty just little star dots. With nothing but dotted stars in view I
> couldn't tell where I was, what I already looked at before, was I looking up or down sideways or
> backwards. There's no time to process anything, no landmarks to tell where I was. I also felt
> like it was way too fast. If this is the meat of the game, this needs to last a lot longer."

Their suggestions, which are the requirements in §2:

* a "tracking line" — a subtle yellow rail leading to the planet way in the distance, showing the
  zig-zag the ship takes;
* triple the length of the flight, at least;
* limit the player's ability to look fully down or fully overhead;
* keep buttons simple, and say on screen what to press to photograph or focus;
* something in the background marking the horizontal plane — "maybe like a subtle gold-ish aurora
  borealis waves that effectively act as the horizon line" — so that even when not facing the
  planet you know whether you are looking too high or low. **They are open to an alternative here**
  as long as it removes the disorientation; the existing little pointer is not enough;
* the destination planet is way too big and distracting — keep it far away, and end the flight
  before it fills the screen;
* you should be able to **see what you are trying to photograph while it is still distant**, so you
  can turn toward it, judge whether it is high or low, and plan — perhaps zooming to get the shot.

## 1. Why the sky was empty — the measurement

Four facts, all read from the code of the flown build
(`.astro_scratch/safari5-0921/safari5-review/proj`):

1. **A sight is only drawn once you are nearly pointing at it.** `safari_run.gd:1012` appends a
   subject to `_live` only when `d < 1.65` field radii, and `SafariCatalog.FIELD_HALF_DEG = 7.0`
   (`safari_catalog.gd:109`). So nothing is drawn beyond **11.5 degrees** from the crosshair.
2. **The sky you can point at is enormous by comparison.** Azimuth is unbounded (360 deg) and
   elevation is clamped to `EL_MIN -52.0` / `EL_MAX 56.0` (`safari_run.gd:90-91`) — a band of about
   360 x 108 degrees. The drawn disc is roughly **1 percent** of it. Swinging blind cannot work.
3. **The only instruction fades away.** `safari_cockpit.gd:341-347` draws
   "drag to swing · knob for range · tap the shutter" at 13*k px along the very bottom edge and
   fades it out over the first 9 seconds. Nothing else in the flight ever says what a control does.
4. **The flight is too short to learn anything.** The two flown runs were `run=62.0s` and
   `run=52.0s` (the player's own session log, 2026-09-21).

The player's log also shows the consequence exactly: for all 12 sights across both flights,
`seen=0.00` — and `seen` is the *maximum centred-ness ever reached* (`safari_run.gd:1018`), not a
duration. The crosshair never came near a single sight.

**Lesson, for `docs/OPEN_ISSUES.md`:** five rounds of agent measurement graded this loop as working.
Every gate was scored *after* pointing the scope at a known subject. Not one asked the only question
that mattered — **can a person who has never seen this find anything at all?** A loop must be graded
by a first-time run with no prior knowledge, not by a probe that already knows where to look.

## 2. The requirements

Each one is written so a critic can pass or fail it from a frame or a number.

### R1 — You can see a sight before you are pointing at it

Every sight that is "up" must be visible somewhere in the view as soon as it is within the player's
reachable sky, not only within 11.5 degrees of the crosshair. Far away it is a small, dim, uncoloured
mark; as it comes toward the centre and into focus it resolves into the real thing.

* **Gate:** from a standing start with the crosshair at the lane's default heading, a frame must
  contain a visible mark for at least **half of the sights that are up at that moment**, and their
  on-screen positions must agree with each sight's true azimuth/elevation to within 2 degrees.
* **Gate:** a far mark must never be mistakable for a star. State the measured luma and size
  difference against the starfield in the same frame.
* The distant mark does not score. Exposure still needs centring and focus, exactly as now.

### R2 — You always know which way is level

A wide, soft band across the sky marks the flight's horizontal plane — the user's "gold-ish aurora
borealis waves". It must be readable at every heading, including with your back to both planets.

* **Gate:** in frames taken at 8 headings 45 degrees apart, and at elevations -40, 0 and +40, a
  critic who is shown only the frame must be able to say whether the view is above, on, or below the
  plane, and be right every time.
* It must stay subtle: it is a horizon, not a light show. Palette gates in `docs/STYLE_GUIDE.md` hold
  (no swatch above S 0.60).
* An alternative to the aurora is allowed **only** if it passes the same gate and the lead approves
  it against a frame. The existing pointer does not count.

### R3 — The route ahead is drawn

A subtle gold rail shows the path the ship is taking, curving and rising and falling toward the
destination, so the flight reads as a journey with a shape rather than a drift.

* **Gate:** the rail is visible in a frame at the start, the middle and the end of a run, and its
  far end meets the destination planet.
* **Gate:** the rail must not cross the bullseye often enough to spoil shots. State the fraction of
  run-seconds in which the rail passes within CENTRE_R of the crosshair; it must be under 10%.

### R4 — The flight lasts at least three times longer

Runs become **at least 3x** the flown 52-62 s — a target of **170-190 real seconds**. The cast is
spread over the longer run; the player gets time to look, plan and turn.

* **Gate:** ten runs across different lanes report a run length in the target band.
* **Gate:** a flight still costs about the same slice of the game day. The 20-minute day and the
  2-to-3-flights-a-day rhythm (user ruling, `docs/CORE_LOOP.md`) are unchanged, so the real-seconds
  cost of a flight rises while `PHOTO_TRIP_HOURS` stays put — say which constant absorbed it.
* **Gate:** nothing becomes unwinnable or trivial. Report catches-per-run before and after, with the
  spread, on the same seeds.

### R5 — You cannot look straight up or straight down

Tighten the elevation clamp so the player never faces an empty pole. The lead sets no number: the
builder proposes one and proves it with frames showing that the reachable sky still contains every
sight of a full cast.

### R6 — The controls say what they do, and never stop saying it

A permanent, readable legend for the controls. The 9-second fade is deleted.

* **Gate:** a frame taken at run second 5, 60 and 170 all show what to press to photograph and to
  focus.
* **Gate:** measured on a real 2556x1179 `--ui=mobile` frame, the legend's text is at least 22 device
  px tall and sits clear of the porthole and of both thumbs' rest zones.
* Keep the controls simple. If a control cannot be labelled in two or three words, it is too clever.

### R7 — The destination planet is small and far

The planet must read as a destination, not a wall.

* **Gate:** at every second of a run, the destination planet covers no more than **12%** of the
  porthole's area, and the run ends before it grows past that.
* **Gate:** it is still clearly there and clearly growing — state its on-screen radius at the start,
  middle and end of a run.

### R8 — No straight lines: the route winds and changes height

Added by the user, 2026-09-21: "no straight line routes. Use winding paths, maybe some elevation
etc. Research inspiration from Pokemon Snap maps." A flight is a journey with a shape, and the shape
is what makes one lane feel unlike another. R3's rail is how you *see* the route; R8 is the route
itself.

* **Gate:** a top-down and a side-on plot of every lane's path. No lane is a straight line, and no
  lane's path is within 5 degrees of straight for more than 25% of its run-seconds.
* **Gate:** every lane changes height. State each lane's total climb and dive in degrees of pitch
  and the largest single change.
* **Gate:** the turns and climbs are *used* — for each lane, say which sights only become visible
  because of a turn or a height change, and show the frame before and after.
* **Gate:** the shape does not fight the camera. Report how much the horizon band (R2) rolls, and
  cap it: the player must never feel the ship is upside down.
* **Gate:** eight lanes, eight recognisable shapes. A critic shown only the eight path plots,
  unlabelled, must be able to match them to the eight lane names after one described run each.
* Pokemon Snap is the stated reference. The lead will paste the research findings into §5 of this
  file before the builders start; builders build against §5, not against their own memory of the
  game.

## 3. Rules that do not change

* The round porthole with the ship's inside around it (user ruling). No rectangular view.
* Look A, the brass scope, with the picture blooming as you hold (user ruling).
* "Just travelling" and "Photo time!" at the pad; film per trip; a flight costs real clock time.
* The 20-minute day, 10 of it dark, and 2 to 3 photo flights a day (user ruling).
* Everything procedural. No imported art. No new asset pipeline.
* Compatibility renderer is the phone. Any frame used as evidence must say which renderer produced
  it, and the heat budget is judged on Compatibility.

## 4. How this will be graded

* **No probe that already knows where a sight is may be used as the pass gate for R1 or R2.** The
  evidence is frames, plus a run driven by a first-time policy that only uses what is on screen.
* Every claim carries a frame or a number, and says which renderer and which resolution produced it.
* Round cap 2. If a requirement still fails after two rounds, stop and tell the lead.

## 5. Route research — Pokemon Snap and friends (filled by the lead, 2026-09-21)

Gathered by a research agent from Pokemon Snap (N64, 1999), New Pokemon Snap (2021) and, for pacing
only, theme-park dark rides. **Sourcing honesty:** the two Wikipedia articles and the Game Developer
piece are solid; the course beats come from fan walkthroughs and guide sites, which are good for
"what is in the course" and weak for "why"; **no official route diagram and no per-course duration
could be verified**, and the community says almost nothing about verticality, so R8's elevation is
our own call, not Snap's. Anything below marked *(inference)* is the researcher's reading, not a
sourced claim.

The eleven rules below are the contract. **S1-S3 and S9 are the ones that answer the user's report**;
build those first if anything has to be cut.

* **S1 — the first shot cannot miss.** One slow, large subject, dead ahead and fully visible inside
  the first 5 seconds. Snap's first course opens with two Pidgey straight ahead; the difficulty of
  the target teaches, not a text box (GameFAQs walkthrough).
* **S2 — the second subject is off to one side**, inside the next 10 seconds, so the player's second
  action is to *turn*, not to repeat. (Beach: a Doduo leaps in from the left, then a distant Lapras.)
* **S3 — every subject gets a 1-2 second warning before it can be framed**: a sound plus a mark at
  the porthole edge. New Snap gives an explicit on-screen directional prompt; the warning is UI, not
  luck (Game8). This is R1's other half, and it is what the collar dabs become once they carry
  elevation.
* **S4 — cut the run into 7 to 9 segments of 15-25 seconds**, each with its own backdrop, instead of
  one continuous drift. At our 156-234 s that is 8 or 9 segments. (Dark rides run 3-6 minutes built
  from discrete show scenes — Blooloop, Wikipedia.)
* **S5 — one nameable landmark per segment**, on the route itself, so a player can say *where* they
  saw a thing, not only *when*.
* **S6 — alternate busy and quiet segments about 1:1**, a busy one being 3 or more subjects up at
  once and a quiet one 0 or 1. Never more than two busy segments in a row.
* **S7 — one subject per run sits behind the start heading**, and the ship slows by about 30% while
  the player looks more than 90 degrees off heading. Snap's vehicle slows on a look-back so the
  course buys the player time rather than demanding prediction (converging guide sources). If the
  slow-down fights R4's run length, keep the behind-you subject and drop the slow-down, and say so.
* **S8 — the biggest set piece lands in the last 10-15% of the run.** Both games save the one
  scripted spectacle for the end of a course (Volcano's finale).
* **S9 — keep a fixed piece of the ship in frame as the "this way is forward" anchor.** The vehicle
  interior is the one constant reference frame in both games (Wikipedia). We already have the amber
  nose wedge (`safari_cockpit.gd:281-290`) — it must survive the wide field and stay readable.
* **S10 — at most one detour per run, on a single trigger**, and a detour changes *what appears*, not
  where the ship flies. New Snap's multi-step, cross-day branch chains need dozens of return visits;
  a 3-minute procedural run cannot carry a fork a player can permanently miss.
* **S11 — one full-length vertical arc per lane**, not a staircase of little climbs. The reference
  games use elevation once and deliberately *(inference — the community is near-silent on it)*. The
  user asked for winding paths with elevation, so R8 wins on the winding: **the route turns often in
  azimuth, and climbs or dives once in a big arc**, plus at most two smaller changes.

**Not stolen, and why:** the 60-photo film cap is a cartridge-era constraint, not a design; the
tool-unlock replay loop needs an inventory and dozens of hand-built courses; hand-authored spectacle
beats are bespoke animation a generator cannot place; and full free-look with auto-slow compensation
is tuned for a TV held in two hands, so it may fight a porthole on a phone.

## 6. Lead rulings after the code map (2026-09-21)

A read-only Opus map of the flight corrected §1 and settled the questions the builders cannot settle
for themselves. Every ruling here is part of the contract.

### 6.1 Corrections to §1

* **The `1.65` cull is not what hides the sky — the porthole is.** `_win` is square, so
  `window_aspect()` returns 1.0 (`safari_run.gd:748-749`) and `CORNER_Q := 1.0` (`:136`); the
  eyepiece SDF collapses to a circle of **one field radius** (`safari_eyepiece.gdshader:1037-1040`).
  The glass shows a **14-degree circle**. The `1.65` gate is looser than what can be drawn, so
  raising it alone would change nothing. R1 must change the field, not the cull.
* **The true number is worse than "1 percent".** Visible cap 2*pi*(1-cos 7deg) = 0.0468 sr against a
  reachable sky of 10.16 sr: **0.46%, one part in 217**, and 26 porthole-widths to go round in
  azimuth.
* **Runs are 52-78 s, not 52-62** (`safari_lanes.gd:296,312,327,342,357,373,388,405`).
* **There is already a pointer, and it is half-blind.** The collar dabs
  (`safari_cockpit.gd:267-279`) mark every uncaught sight on the porthole ring — but by **azimuth
  only**. Elevation never reaches it. That is exactly the user's "the little pointer isn't enough".
* **A fourth invisible feature.** `safari_run.gd:477-492` builds an additive billboard per sight at
  300 m and positions it every frame (`:510-528`). Nothing 3D survives during a run: the opaque
  shader disc plus the opaque 2D hull cover the screen. Those quads have never been seen by anyone.
  Delete them; the marks are done in the shader instead.
* **Landmine, brief every builder:** the shader reads `field_half`
  (`safari_eyepiece.gdshader:143`), which the flight never sets, and ignores `field_deg`, which the
  flight does set (`safari_run.gd:1047`). Changing `SafariCatalog.FIELD_HALF_DEG` moves the scoring
  and the collar and **leaves the picture identical**. `field_half` must be wired from GDScript.

### 6.2 The central ruling: a wide window, and a scope you raise

R1 is not solved with a radar ring. The player asked to *see the thing in the distance and turn to
it*, and they said "I may need to zoom in to get a picture". So:

* **Lowered (the default): a wide view.** `field_half = 28 deg` — a **56-degree** window. Shapes
  drift past as small, soft, low-contrast forms. This is where you look around, read the aurora,
  follow the rail and decide what to go for. It is 18x more sky than today (8.3% of reachable, not
  0.46%).
* **Raised: the scope.** `field_half = 7 deg`, exactly today's glass, for the shot.
* **One simple control switches them.** The builder proposes it; the critic grades it against R6.
  Four controls is the ceiling: swing, scope, focus, shutter — each labelled in one word.
* **Scoring does not change, and this is the point.** `CENTRE_R 0.30` and `EDGE_R 0.78` stop being
  fractions of a variable field and become the **absolute degrees they are tuned to today: a 2.1 deg
  bullseye and a 5.5 deg edge.** Five rounds of exposure, hold and clash tuning survive untouched.
  Because the bullseye is 2.1 deg inside a 56 deg window, you *must* raise the scope to aim — which
  is the feel we want, and it is Pokemon Snap's feel.
* **The lead applies the `field_half` wiring and the absolute-degree scoring conversion before the
  groups start**, because it crosses G1 and G3.

| run seconds | 52-78 | **x3: 156-234** (each lane's own value x3) |
| sights per run | 6-7 (5 slots) | **14-18** (slots scale to match; density eases from one per 9.3 s to one per ~12 s) |
| `PHOTO_TRIP_HOURS` | 4.9 | **6.0** — 234 s + 6 pad + 10 haul = 5.92 h in daylight, so the flat charge still covers the longest lane. 2-3 flights a day is unchanged (3 x 6.0 = 18 h) |

* `SLOT_F` is five fixed fractions and `draw_cast` emits at most 7 entries **whatever `seconds` is**

  count is part of the same change.
* `_dest_start = DEST_END + LANE_SPEED * (run + pad)` (`space_lane.gd:201`) reaches 9211 m at 3x,
  past the camera's `far = 4000.0` (`safari_run.gd:465`). Raise `far` and retune the approach table
  at `space_lane.gd:22-28`. R7's 12%-of-area cap is judged on the new wide field.

## 7. Lead rulings after wave 1 (2026-09-22)

G1 (the glass) and G2 (the cabin) both reached the round cap at FAIL. **Most of what failed was this
document, not the builders.** Their own files are done and independently re-measured. The rulings:

### 7.1 R3's second gate was measuring the wrong thing — amended

The gate said "the fraction of run-seconds in which the rail passes within CENTRE_R of the
crosshair, under 10%". Three lanes read 12.0-14.6% by that arithmetic (fen_grig 14.6, grig_vela
14.5, hub_bolt 12.0) — **while painting nothing at all in the bullseye.** The critic pinned the
crosshair on the rail on two lanes and diffed rail-on against `--no-rail`: **0 of 1969 pixels inside
the 2.10 deg bullseye moved by more than 0.004 luma** (max residual 0.0039, dither), against
13,000-16,900 changed rail pixels outside it as the positive control. The keep-out works; the arc
measurement counted geometry the renderer never draws.

**Amended gate:** zero rail pixels inside CENTRE_R, measured as a rail-on vs `--no-rail` pixel diff
on at least three lanes, each with the positive control outside the bullseye stated. The
arc-seconds figure may be reported, but it does not gate.

### 7.2 R1's standing-start gate was unsatisfiable — amended, and the real fix moves to G3

At t=0.62 with the crosshair at the lane default, **five of eight lanes have exactly one sight up,
and it is `limb_*_day_behind` at azimuth 157 degrees — behind the player.** Three lanes have nothing
up at all. No change inside G1's files could pass "half the up sights visible in one standing-start
frame", because the content is not there to see.

**Amended gate:** the deliberate behind-you sight of S7 is excluded from R1's standing-start count.
**And the actual requirement moves to G3 as S1, which it always was:** every lane opens with one
slow, large subject dead ahead and fully visible inside the first 5 seconds, so the first shutter
press cannot miss. R1 is then graded on the sights that are up *in front*.

### 7.3 Section 6.4 misassigned the film stack — corrected

The film plates are drawn in `safari_run.gd:624-710`, which is **G1's** file, not G2's. G2 could not
have passed that gate from `safari_cockpit.gd`. The deliverable moves to whoever owns
`safari_run.gd`, and the 49-device-px plate beside a 668-px porthole is still open.

### 7.4 R6's timestamps assumed work that had not landed — amended

"A frame at run second 5, 60 and 170" cannot be met while every lane tops out at 78 s. **Amended:**
a frame at 5 s, at the run's midpoint, and inside the last 10 s of the run, whatever its length.

### 7.5 One real new defect, carried into wave 2

G2's elevation chevron **clips off the bottom edge of the porthole frame** when an uncaught sight is
near dead-astern (about 180 deg) and below the player. Reproduced on 2 of 7 real captures. Round cap
reached, so it goes to a wave-2 builder rather than a third G2 round.

### 7.6 An honest measurement that changes what R1 means

On the merged build, one lane reports: 24.8 sight-seconds up; **10.9 s on the glass under the old
11.5-degree gate, 12.4 s inside the new 56-degree window — 44% to 50% of up-time.** The wide window
alone is a **small** gain, because the cast is spread across a full 360 degrees and most sights are
simply not in front of you. What answers the user's complaint is the *pair*: the wide window and the
far marks for what is ahead, and the collar ring with elevation for everything else. Any future round
that weakens the collar on the grounds that "the window is wide now" is wrong, and this is the number
that says so.

### 7.7 Known scaffold to remove

`safari_run.gd` prints `SAFARI R5 bridge: N cast elevations pulled into -26..26` every run: G1's
clamp is pulling cast elevations into the tighter limits because `safari_lanes.gd:43-44` still holds
the old copy. **G3 must make the lane table fit inside the new limits, at which point the bridge
becomes a no-op.** It must not be left as the thing that makes R5 true.

## 8. Lead rulings after wave 2 (2026-09-22)

G3 (the lane), G4 (the ledger) and G6 (papercuts) all PASSED. The headline measurement is G3's
critic's, not the builder's: it wrote its own **first-time-player simulator** — one that can only see
what is inside the current window, takes 0.40 s to commit to a target, slews at the game's own
55 deg/s cap, and scores with `safari_run.gd`'s exact formula — and ran 168 runs on each build.

| build | catches per run | runs that caught NOTHING |
|---|---|---|
| before (wave 1 merge) | 0.88 | **67 of 168** |
| after (G3) | 11.30 | **0 of 168** |

The 67-of-168 figure reproduces the real player's report from a program, which is the gate §4 asked
for and never had. **That simulator is now project equipment**: no future round of this loop passes
on a probe that already knows where the cast is.

### 8.1 S4 contradicted itself — segment length wins

S4 said "8 or 9 segments" *and* "15-25 seconds each". At 234 s both cannot hold (234/9 = 26.0).
**Amended: segment length 15-25 s is the rule, and the count follows — 8 to 10 segments.** `longhome`
runs 10 segments of 23.4 s and that is correct.

### 8.2 S6 is not met. Deferred on purpose, with the number

Measured, scope-independent: on all five 8-segment lanes the busy/quiet pattern is **six quiet
segments, one busy**, with three segments at one-or-fewer sights for 100% of their seconds.
`longhome` has **zero** busy segments. S6 asked for about 1:1.

The critic showed the builder's stated reason (that S6 fights R4's 14-18 cap) is wrong: what blocks
it is that every non-clash window is hard-wired to exactly one segment in `draw_cast`. So it is
fixable.

**Ruled: leave it until the user has flown this.** The complaint being fixed was an empty sky, and
that went from 67-runs-empty to none. Adding pressure before a human has said the calm version feels
good risks trading a fixed problem for a new one. Revisit after the next real play.

### 8.3 The film ratio in §6.3 was wrong — the text is corrected, the numbers stand

§6.3 claimed 10 plates "keeps today's tuned ratio of about 0.8 plates per sight". Against a 17-18
sight cast that is **0.56**, not 0.8. The numbers G4 shipped (base 10, buyable to 14, upgradeable to
16) are kept and the claim is withdrawn: **you can now photograph a little over half of what you
see, and choosing what to let go is the point.** The "Let go" list on the haul card is the feature,
not a failure state. `FILM_UPGRADE_COSTS` (400/850 scrap) is now a real balance question and is
deferred to an economy pass, not guessed at here.

### 8.4 S5's landmarks exist in the data and nowhere on the screen — wave 3

`SafariLanes.segments_for()` returns a distinct written landmark name per segment, with t0/t1 and
roles. **Nothing ever draws one.** That is the other half of the user's report — *"no landmarks to
tell where I was"* — and it is still unanswered in the picture. It goes to wave 3 as a cabin job.

### 8.5 R8 is confirmed deferred to wave 3

Nothing in wave 2 delivered R8. The route shape lives in `space_lane.gd` (the ship's path), not in
the cast tables, so it belongs with G5 — and it had to wait for the run length to settle, which it
now has at 156-234 s.

### 8.6 The self-test is measuring the wrong thing — fix it before trusting it again

`tools/safari_selftest.gd` fails two items and **both failures are the self-test's own**: `_item2`
counts sights whose *time windows* overlap and calls that "on the glass", never looking at azimuth

## 9. The second real play, and the rulings it forced (2026-09-22)

The user flew the wave-2 build and came back with **1 catch out of 17**, against the 11.30 that the
first-time-player simulator predicted. The gap is the finding.

### 9.1 What the log proved

* **The focus knob is a hidden wall.** `_focus` starts at 0.5 (`safari_run.gd:251`) and
  `sharp = 1 - |_focus - e.focus| / FOCUS_TOL` with `FOCUS_TOL = 0.30` (`:118`, `:1441`). Since
  `q = centred * sharp` and `hold` only grows while `q >= Q_FLOOR`, a sight whose focus is more than
  0.30 from the knob **can never be photographed at all**. Measured across the catalog's 51 focus
  values (range 0.18 to 1.00): **19 of 51 — 37% — are impossible at the starting knob**, and 9 more
  sit below sharp 0.33. Perfect aim does nothing for any of them.
* **The player's log shows exactly that.** Six sights recorded `seen = 1.00` — dead centre, perfectly
  aimed — with `hold = 0.00`. The one catch, at t=5.4, happened only because S1's opening sight sits
  near the default knob by luck.
* **The shutter is dead on arrival.** `_fire_shutter()` returns "nothing exposing" unless
  `hold / hold_sec > 0.02`, i.e. unless the shot is *already* running. The player pressed it
  **110 times** and got nothing, with no feedback about why.
* **The miss reasons lie.** A centred-but-unfocused sight falls through `_miss_reason()` to
  "found it too late", which teaches the player to hurry — the opposite of the truth, which is
  "you never got it sharp". Printed six times in one flight.

### 9.2 Why the simulator missed it — the lesson

G3's critic wrote, in its own report, that its first-time policy **"finds the focus knob in 0.6 s"**,
and listed it under *unproven*. The lead read that and shipped anyway. **A caveat a critic files
under "unproven" about the thing being tested is a blocker, not a footnote.** The sim was a better
model than a probe that knows the cast, and it was still wrong in exactly the place it warned about.

### 9.3 R9 — the shutter always does something (user ruling, points 3 and 4)

The user chose auto-focus and hold-to-shoot, and then said the root of their confusion plainly:
*"I was expecting the shutter to click, not for me to need to hold anything."* Both are honoured:

* **Tap the shutter: you get the picture, now**, graded on how good the shot was at that instant.
* **Hold the shutter: the picture blooms** to a better grade, which is the look the user already
  chose. Let go, or run out of subject, and you keep what you had.
* **No press is ever dead.** If nothing is in the glass at all, say so on screen in words — never
  silence.
* **Focus glides automatically** to whatever sits nearest the crosshair. The knob stays, as an
  optional twist for extra sharpness and a better grade. Nothing in the catalog may be unreachable
  without it.
* **The miss card tells the truth.** A sight that was centred but never sharp says so. No message may
  name a cause the numbers do not support.
* **GATE, and it replaces the old one:** the critic writes a **tap-only first-time policy** — one
  that never touches the focus knob, never holds the shutter, and only taps while roughly aimed —
  and it must average at least 5 catches per run across every lane, with no run catching zero. The
  previous sim, which found the knob in 0.6 s, is no longer an acceptable pass gate.

### 9.4 R10 — the rail is a railway under the ship (user ruling, point 1)

*"Can you make the light trail leading the planet seemingly more directly underneath the ship? It's
so far below it feels like it's leading to something below me vs a railway I'm riding on."*

The rail must read as track the ship is running along: close under the hull, converging toward the
destination, not a line slung far below aimed at something else. **Gate:** in frames at the start,
middle and end of a run at the default heading, the rail's near end sits within a stated small angle
below the crosshair and its far end meets the destination; a critic shown only the frames says
"I am riding on this", not "that is below me". The bullseye keep-out of §7.1 still holds.

### 9.5 R11 — space must look like this game (user ruling, point 2)

*"Space looks too realistic which is different than the art style we've established in the game
today."* The starfield and sky are photoreal and the rest of the game is not. Restyle them to the
game's own look: the Moonstone palette, the flat cozy shapes, the thin-atmosphere space look of
`docs/STYLE_GUIDE.md`. **Gate:** a frame of the flight and a frame of a planet surface, side by side,
must read as the same game — stated as a palette measurement plus a critic's blind judgement. The
user's words were "more inspired by Animal Crossing"; read that as *cozy and stylised, not
photoreal*, which is what `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §1.1 already asks for. Do not copy Animal Crossing.

### 9.6 Open, and NOT a builder's job — the story (user points 5 and 6)

*"Starting a new game and going right into safari with no context of why I'm doing it didn't make
sense story wise"* and *"the ship was showing as damaged in a new game? I thought we were going to
get rid of that part of the story to repair the ship? Are we now repairing the ship and also
strengthening our telescope/camera?"*

Both are real and neither has been decided. The game currently has **two progression ladders** — the
five rocket parts of the old campaign, and the camera and film upgrades of the safari — and no story
reason to fly at all. This is the campaign-spine rewrite that `docs/CORE_LOOP.md` has been waiting
on, and it goes to the user as a decision before anything is built.

## 10. User rulings, 2026-09-22 (the story spine, and three flight changes)

### 10.1 THE STORY SPINE IS RULED: "photograph your way home", with the neighbours kept

The user chose story option 2 and then improved it:

> "I would go with story 2, but I don't want to lose the mini-game quests for the neighbors so I
> want this photograph request to be the last thing they want before they give the part you need
> for the ship."

So the shape is **not** "replace the neighbour quests with a photo". It is:

1. A neighbour's existing chain of favours and mini-games runs **exactly as it does today**.
2. At the **end** of that chain, instead of handing over the ship part, the neighbour asks for **one
   photograph** — a named thing, out in the sky, that you must go and find.
3. You fly, hunt it, bring it back. **Then** you get the part.

This keeps everything already built — the five-part ladder, the favours, the mini-games, Norm, the
finale — and it finally gives the sky a consequence, which has been the missing piece since the
safari was first proposed. It also answers the user's two complaints from the second play: a new game
now has a reason to fly, and there is **one** ladder, not two.

**Still to design (lead, before any builder):** which five sights are the asked-for photographs and
how they are gated so the player can actually reach them; what the neighbour says; what happens if
the player photographs the thing *before* being asked; and how the ask is recorded so it survives a
reload. `docs/CORE_LOOP.md` and `docs/BUILD_PLAN.md` both need amending, not just this file.

### 10.2 R12 — remove the wavy planet effect

> "the wavy effect of the planet is disorienting so i say we remove it."

The destination and home globes are drawn with a swim/wave distortion (the eyepiece's `swim`
parameter and the lane globe shaders). **Remove it.** Gate: frames before and after at the same pose,
plus a statement of what else used the same parameter, since `swim` is shared with other glass
effects and must not be ripped out from under them.

### 10.3 R13 — a clock in the cabin, and a way to go faster

> "A timer might also be good to show somewhere during the safari with a speed up button to boost
> the ship faster in case people are hunting one specific photograph."

Two parts, both new:

* **A clock.** The player can see how much of the flight is left, at a glance, at any moment. It must
  survive the R6 legend rules: readable at 2556x1179 `--ui=mobile`, clear of the porthole and both
  thumb zones, and it never fades.
* **A boost.** A control that speeds the ship up, for a player who has what they came for, or who is
  hunting one specific thing and wants to get to it. Design questions the builder must answer with
  measurements, not opinion: does boosting **skip** sights or merely compress them; does it cost
  anything; can it be held or is it a toggle; and what it does to the flight's game-hour cost, which
  is fixed at `PHOTO_TRIP_HOURS = 6.0` and must not silently change.
* **The interaction with 10.1 matters most.** Once a neighbour has asked for a named photograph, a
  player who has already got it should be able to cut the rest of the flight short. Boost is the
  answer to "I only came for one thing", so it must not punish that.
* This makes five controls where §2 R6 set a ceiling of four (swing, scope, focus, shutter). **Lead
  ruling: the ceiling rises to five, and boost is the fifth**, because it is the user's own request
  and it buys back time rather than spending attention. No sixth without another user ruling.

## 11. R14 — the first photo flight teaches itself (user ruling, 2026-09-22)

> "the game should have a tutorial where it should pause multiple times to show you:
> * that its viewable in the distance
> * how to zoom in
> * how to hold and snap the photo
> * the number of limited camera shots you have"

The first photo flight — which the story now makes the trip to the Commons with the Professor's

| # | Pause fires when | It shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the asked sight's far mark is first up in the wide window | **You can see it from far away.** Point at the far mark, and at the collar dab that says which way and how high |
| 2 | that sight is inside the wide window and near enough to zoom on | **Zoom in.** Point at the scope control |
| 3 | the sight is in the raised scope | **Tap for a quick snap, or hold and let it bloom.** Point at the shutter |
| 4 | the first plate is used | **You have a limited number of shots.** Point at the film plates: "that used 1 of your 10" |

  is missed while reading. It resumes on a tap. A paused flight must not bill game hours.

* **Once, and skippable.** Recorded in a flag so it never plays again; a "skip tutorial" choice on
  the first card; a way to replay it from settings is welcome but not required.
* If the player does something out of order — zooms before pause 2, shoots before pause 3 — that

* **Gate:** the tap-only first-time policy of §9.3, run through this flight with the tutorial on,

### 12.1 G5b — the near rail, re-diagnosed

Round 2 fixed the far tangle with a quadratic Bezier from the ship to the destination's **centre,
kilometres away**. That far end dominates the curve within the first 10-20 m, so **the rails under
the hull swing toward the planet instead of following the ship's line**: measured every 0.1 s on all
42 trips, the track under the crosshair sits 10-18 degrees to one side for **10.5% of run-time (up to
25% on the Long Way Home)**. And the pull point jumps when a local minimum appears or disappears, so
the near track **pops sideways 1-6 times a run, up to 4.6 degrees in one frame**. Start and end
frames look right, which is why the builder's own start/middle/end sheets missed both.

**The fix, from the critic's own measurement:** draw the **true route** for the near ~100 m (0 tangles
at a 105 m reach, and the near track within 1.7 degrees of straight below), then bend to the planet
with a curve whose direction matches the route where they join, from a join point that is smoothed
or rate-limited so it cannot jump. **Gates:** sampled every 0.1 s on all 42 trips at the default
heading, the near track's centre line within 5 degrees of straight below for at least 98% of
samples; no frame-to-frame jump above 1 degree anywhere; 0 tangles; the far end meets the disc; the
keep-out holds. And **sheets chosen by measurement, not only start/middle/end** — the moments the
probe flags as worst.

### 12.2 Boost is built and hidden, until the bill is right

G7 built boost, but a boosted flight changed the bill, so it ships with `BOOST_LIVE = false`. The
cause is outside G7's files: `environment.gd::_charge_absence` bills real wall seconds for time
away, and `safari_flight.gd::_on_landed` only trues up `_extra_hours_total` — so a shorter wall time
bills fewer hours. **Ruling: `PHOTO_TRIP_HOURS = 6.0` is the whole flight, from departure to landing
including the haul card, boosted or not.** The probe's 12 s of ground between legs is the probe's,
not the flight's. **G4b** makes that exact; the lead flips `BOOST_LIVE` at merge once a boosted and
an unboosted ledger both read 6.0.

### 12.3 Ruling: no photo without a press

G7's critic found that **passive exposure still catches on its own**: a player who never touches the
shutter but keeps a sight centred fills all 10 plates. That is the old hold-still mechanic left
running under R9. The user's words were *"I was expecting the shutter to click"*, and the tutorial
they asked for teaches "how to hold and snap the photo". **A picture is taken only by the shutter.**
Keeping a sight centred still builds the bloom, so a patient tap is a better picture — but the
camera never shoots by itself. The tap-only gate must still pass after this change.

### 12.4 R8 reworded — turns show in the world, not in the cast

Gate R8 asked for "sights that only become visible because of a turn". G5's critic showed that
cannot be met by the route alone: **sights are placed and aimed in the ship's frame**
(`safari_lanes.gd`, `safari_cast.gd`, `safari_run.gd`), so a turn moves the world, not the cast.
Moving the cast into the world frame would break the bit-identical scoring every round has depended
on, and would make the sky harder to read, not easier. **Amended:** the route's turns and climbs must
be visible in the world features — the rail, the destination's bearing drifting across the glass,
and the band sliding through a turn. The "sights revealed by a turn" gate is withdrawn. The
straightness gate's window is fixed at **20 seconds** (turn rates top out near 1-1.5 deg/s; a 10 s
window fails every lane, a 30 s window passes almost anything).

### 12.5 R2 ruled — the band marks the ship's level

Round 2 ties the band to the ship's own plane, so it cannot roll. **That is the right level**: it is
the plane the sights live in and the plane the look clamp limits, which is exactly what the user
asked it to tell them — *"if I'm looking too high or low to see something"*. The world's own level
can lean up to 11.9 degrees on the steepest lane and is shown by the rail and the planet instead.
The readability bar is the **phone-scale** frame, not a thumbnail.
